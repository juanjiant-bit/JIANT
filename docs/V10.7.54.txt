V10.7.54 beta — DRUM-X and staccato bass

The missing-bass report can be reproduced with the production sequencer,
DRUM-X (preset 0) and ANALOG FAT BASS (preset 14, factory LEGATO). On each
beat, kick, snare, clap and closed hat start together, followed by hats on
the other sixteenths. A bass note starts on each beat and its release ends
before the next beat. Other parts are empty and unmuted. The earlier
two-track regression reused a sustained synth at mostly fixed deadline load.

The same run spans 64–78% modeled DMA utilization with either firmware.
In V54 the bass actually reaches half rate in 391 of the 399 checked
windows. TRACKS 4 and TRACKS 6 give the same V54 result.

V53 let a note burst keep speculative CPU charges for work already reflected
in the moving average of the DMA deadline. With the kit playing first, the
allocator could deny the return of the bass after a rest. This leaves a
gap in the bass stem even though the kit continues. V54 tracks each part's
measured contribution over the same 4096-frame timescale. At allocation,
only the positive incremental work of existing voices can keep that part's
old charge outstanding. A new slot still incurs its full estimated cost.
The calculation includes active fades and outstanding FX work. Measurements
lose weight during silence and an engine change starts a fresh estimate.
A same-engine patch or rate change accrues credit from subsequent renders.

Voice count and CPU eligibility are now evaluated together, removing a
second pass over sounding voices on the strict admission path. Strict FULL
retains its 78% threshold, per-part caps, adaptive quality and overload
handling. No preset, song or engine DSP parameters were changed.

The host regression uses real sequence timing, the mixer, gate state,
per-note ages and the rendered bass stem. Device ticks are modeled at
80 ticks per part plus 1600 per active voice, with a modeled 25% saving
for eligible reduced synthesis. Each DMA half includes two 64-frame renders;
the longer core's time plus background work supplies its deadline reading.
These figures are not measurements on a physical FM-1.

Live input still has a boundary. With 55% modeled base load, all eight
keyboard/MIDI presses sounded and released. At 59%, seven of eight sounded:
one keyboard request reached 75.90% measured average plus a 2.22% estimate,
above the 78% threshold even with speculative debt removed. Sustained 94%
background load also blocked a new attack. Heavier projects can still lose
notes. Validation of the reported project on a physical FM-1 remains open.

Reproduce: compile tests/measured_admission_test.c as in voice-user-check.yml
and run 'measured-admission bass 6 55' or 'measured-admission bass 6 59'.
The workflow also checks TRACKS 4, expensive patch transitions, active fades,
engine changes, silence, previous two-track and six-track cases, and overload.
