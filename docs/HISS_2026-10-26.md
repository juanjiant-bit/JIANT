# The hiss under a sounding note (owner, 2026-10-26): first measurement

tests/hiss_probe.c (not in the suite): one track alone, a note tied over the bar, the master's output (L) dumped;
`cc -O1 -w -Ibuild/gen -Ifirmware/src -Itests -o hp tests/hiss_probe.c -lm && ENG=0 ./hp DIR 1` (ENG: the engine).
Noise = the median between the harmonics (+-12 Hz) over the band, Blackman, 32768 points.

ANALOG (power-on preset), C3:

| case                    | signal     | noise      | SNR     |
|-------------------------|-----------:|-----------:|--------:|
| LEVEL 104, REV 30 DLY 45 | -11.6 dBFS | -68.9 dBFS | 57.3 dB |
| LEVEL 104, FX 0          | -13.0      | -70.9      | 57.9    |
| LEVEL 40, FX 0           | -44.1      | -89.9      | 45.8    |
| LEVEL 1, FX 0            | -63.8      | -94.6      | 30.9    |
| LEVEL 40, REV / DLY on   | -42.1      | -81.0      | 38.8    |

Reading: (1) most of it scales with the signal (made before the track's fader: the engine, the voice, the part's
filter), ~58 dB under it: about 10 bits, a soft bit-crusher's; (2) a floor of ~-95 dBFS after the fader (the mix /
master: ~16 bits, fine); (3) the reverb / delay add up to ~9 dB on a quiet part. Not yet separated: ANALOG's own
aliasing (inharmonic, counted here as noise) from the rounding. Next: a pure sine (no alias) through each stage
(oscillator, voice_amp, the part's filter, the track level, the buses), the stage where the floor jumps fixed
(rounding instead of truncating, or more headroom bits there), then the reverb's.
VOICE (power-on T2) has its breath by design: not a measure of the chain.

Later the same day: the owner hears it with every engine. The part's FILTER CUT 127 / 90 / 50 / 20 changes nothing
(57.9 dB each), so not the filter. Left to tell apart on the device: the master volume applied in digital before
the DAC (fewer bits at a low volume), or the output's analog floor (the codec mutes on zeros: the total silence
after the release). The owner's two tests: (A) the FM-1 at full volume, the monitors down; (B) a note at LEVEL ~0.

The owner: it falls with the note (so: the digital chain, before the track's fader). A pure sine (ADD, MODE HARM,
PARTIAL 0: one partial, no alias): SNR 76.4 dB at LEVEL 104 (noise -87.6 dBFS), 56.0 at 40, 44.3 at 1: ~12.5 bits,
still before the fader. ANALOG's 58 dB is that plus its own aliasing. Suspects for next week, in order: the
oscillators' sine / wave tables read without interpolation (SINE[ph >> 22]: 1024 points, ADD / WHEEL and others),
the engines' outputs scaled down before voice_amp (bits lost there, then the levels), voice_amp's two truncations.
The test: the same pure sine through each stage alone, then interpolation / rounding where the floor jumps.

Checked: the uninterpolated table reads (SINE[ph >> 22]) are only ADD's and WHEEL's (eng_add.c, eng_wheel.c); every
other engine reads sine_i (interpolated, its error ~-118 dB). The owner hears the hiss on every engine: so the shared
chain after the oscillators (voice_amp, the voices' sum into the part, the part's scaling before the fader) is the
place to measure first; ADD / WHEEL's interpolation is a second, smaller fix (a multiply a partial a sample: weigh
its CPU).
