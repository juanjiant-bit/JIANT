# JIANT FM — First steps

**For JIANT FM V10.8.48 on the M-VAVE FM-1.** This guide is for someone who has never used a synth or a step
sequencer. It has six short tours. Each one says **what** you will do, **why** (the idea in one sentence) and **how**
(button by button), and ends with an **if it doesn't sound, or you're lost** tip. The names in **bold** are exactly the
ones the device shows. The full detail is in the [manual](MANUAL.md).

## Before you start: the panel on one page

**Tracks.** JIANT has tracks, and each one has its own sound and its own sequence. At power-on four of them play:
**T1** a drum kit (DRUM-X), **T2** ANALOG, **T3** PHASE and **T4** FM6 (a bass). T5 and T6 stay asleep until you set
TRACKS to 6.

**The big knobs**

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **ALGORITHM** | Picks the track: T1, T2, T3, T4. Everything you touch after that belongs to that track. |
| **PRESETS** | Changes the track's sound. On the **SEQ** pages it moves the step cursor instead. |
| **SELECT** | Walks the pages of the section you are in, one page a click. |
| **KNOB 1–4** | The four knobs under the screen. Each one moves the card drawn above it. |
| **MASTER** | The volume. |

**The buttons**

- **Section buttons**: **HOME**, **EDIT** (the sound), **SEQ** (the sequencer), **FX** (effects), **GLO** (mix and
  tempo), **ENV**, **LFO**, **SCL** (the scale), **ARP** and **SAVE**. A tap takes you to that section, on the page
  you were last on there. If you are already inside, another tap jumps to its next group of pages.
- **PLAY** starts and stops. **REC** arms recording on the selected track.
- **OCT− / OCT+** move the keyboard's octave down or up. When the screen asks a question, **OCT+** is YES / do it and
  **OCT−** is NO / back.
- **The keys**, F3 to G5, play the selected track.

**The screen**, top to bottom:

- **The header**: at the left, whether it is playing (a triangle) or stopped (a square), and the **BPM** (the tempo).
  A dot means REC is armed. In the middle, the track and its sound (for example **T2 · ANALOG …**), and this is also
  where messages appear ("SAVED", "MUTATED"…). At the right, the octave, a load number (0–99) and the battery.
- **The tabs**: the section's groups. The one you are on is marked, with one dot for each of its pages.
- **The cards**: the name and value of KNOB 1 to KNOB 4.
- **The page's picture**, and at the very bottom **the breadcrumb**: **SECTION · GROUP · page** and its place, for
  example **SEQ · EUCLID · EUCLID**. If you don't know where you are, look there.

**Holding a button opens a layer.** While you hold **GLO**, **SAVE**, **FX**, **EDIT**, **SEQ**, **REC** or **SCL**,
the keys change job, and the screen draws the keyboard with each key's new name on it. Let go and everything is back
to normal. In this guide, "**GLO + G4**" means: hold GLO and press the key G4.

**If you're lost**

- Tap the button of the section you want. **HOME** takes you to the creatures, the home screen.
- **UNDO**: **GLO + G4** undoes the last big change (a clear, the dice, the generator…). Press it again to redo.
- If a note won't stop: hold **PLAY** for almost a second (**PANIC**).
- If the **MENU** opened (it does when you hold HOME alone), tap **HOME** to close it.

## 1. Your first beat with EUCLID

**What.** A whole drum beat in one minute, with three knobs.

**Why.** A euclidean rhythm spreads a number of hits as evenly as possible over a number of steps. For example, 3 hits
over 8 steps give a rhythm heard in music all over the world. You choose how many hits and how many steps, and the
machine does the maths.

**How**

1. Power on. Turn **ALGORITHM** until the header says **T1** (the drums).
2. Press **SEQ** (it opens STEP). Tap **SEQ** twice more, until the **EUCL** tab lights. The breadcrumb reads
   **SEQ · EUCLID · EUCLID**.
3. Check that **KNOB 4** reads **LANE ALL** (the whole kit). Turn **KNOB 1 DENSITY** right, to 3 or 4.
4. Press **PLAY**. You have a kick, a snare and hi-hats.
5. Turn **KNOB 2 STEPS** (the cycle's length) and **KNOB 3 ROTATE**, which shifts the hits and changes the groove.
   Every turn rewrites the pattern while it plays.
6. To work on one sound only, set **KNOB 4** to its lane: **BD** (kick), **SD** (snare), **CH** (closed hat)… Now
   **KNOB 1** is **HITS** and changes that sound alone.
7. Turn **SELECT** one click right: **MODES**. **KNOB 1 ACCENT** adds accents and **KNOB 2 GHOST** adds soft hits in
   between. **OCT+** gives a new variation (**NEW VARIATION**) and **OCT−** goes back to the one before.
8. Turn **SELECT** one click left, back to **EUCLID**. Press and release **OCT+**: the pattern mutates
   (**MUTATED**). **OCT−** goes back to what you dialled with the knobs (**ANCHOR**). For a pattern that changes by
   itself, hold **OCT+** and turn **KNOB 4** to **EVERY 4**: it will mutate every 4 bars.
9. Too fast or too slow? Hold **GLO** and turn **SELECT**: that's the tempo.

**If it doesn't sound, or you're lost**

- Is it playing? The header must show the triangle. Is **MASTER** up? Is **DENSITY** at 0?
- **SONG PLAYS: STOP IT TO EDIT** means a song is playing: press **PLAY** to stop it.
- To get back to EUCLID from anywhere, press **SEQ** (it brings you back to the last SEQ page you were on).
- Don't like the change? **GLO + G4**.

## 2. Your first melody

**What.** A melody and a bassline that always fit, and a sound you like.

**Why.** A scale is a set of notes that sound good together. Once the scale is set, no key sounds "wrong", and EUCLID
can write melodies using only those notes.

**How: the scale**

1. Turn **ALGORITHM** to **T2**.
2. Press **SCL** (the **KEY** page). **KNOB 1 ROOT** is the home note (A, say) and **KNOB 2 SCALE** the kind of
   scale: **MIN** (minor) or **PEN** (pentatonic) are safe to start with.
3. Set **KNOB 3 QUANT** to **WHITE**: the white keys walk the scale and the black keys stay silent. The scale applies
   to every melodic track.

**How: let EUCLID write it**

4. Press **SEQ** (back to EUCLID). On a melodic track **KNOB 1** is **HITS** (how many notes), then **KNOB 2 STEPS**,
   **KNOB 3 ROTATE** and **KNOB 4 ROLE**: **MELODY**, **CHORDS**, **BASS** or **ARP**. Try HITS 5 and ROLE **MELODY**.
5. **SELECT** one click right: **MELODY**. **CONTOUR** gives the melody its shape (UP, ARCH, WAVE…) and **RANGE** how
   many notes it spans. **OCT+** gives another melody with the same settings.
6. For the bass: **ALGORITHM** to **T4**, and on **EUCLID** set **ROLE BASS** and HITS 4.

**How: or record it yourself**

7. Tap **HOME**, pick **T2** with **ALGORITHM** and press **REC**: the dot appears in the header. Press **PLAY**: it
   counts in one bar with a click first, then records what you play. It loops, so you can add more notes on every pass.
   **REC** again stops recording, and the music keeps playing.

**How: the sound**

8. Turn **PRESETS**: every click is another sound. Press **EDIT** to see the engine's knobs. Another tap of **EDIT**
   takes you to **FILTER**, where **KNOB 2 CUT** makes the sound darker or brighter and **KNOB 3 RES** adds a peak.
9. Want a surprise? Hold **EDIT** and turn **SELECT** (the sound dice). **OCT+ OK** keeps it, and **OCT− CANCEL**
   brings back the sound you had.

**If it doesn't sound, or you're lost**

- With **QUANT WHITE**, the black keys are silent on purpose.
- If EUCLID's **HITS** is at 0, the track has no notes.
- If a recording went wrong, **GLO + G4** brings it back to how it was before you armed REC.

## 3. Your first song

**What.** Two versions of your loop, saved, and a list that plays them in order.

**Why.** A **part** (A, B, C or D) is a complete snapshot of the music: the sounds, the steps, the tempo and the
effects. **ARRANGE** is a list of rows, and each row says "this part, for this many bars". That is how a song is built.

**How**

1. **First, an empty project.** At power-on the music starts empty, but **the last project you used** is still open.
   If you save there, you replace what it had. Stop the music, press **SAVE** and turn **SELECT** to **PROJECTS**.
   With **KNOB 1** pick a project whose four A–D chips are dark (empty). Turn **KNOB 2** (**LOAD**) right until its
   card says **OCT+**, and press **OCT+**. Your music stays as it is, and from now on what you save goes into that
   project.
2. Press **PLAY**. Hold **SAVE** and press **C4**: the screen says **SAVED A**.
3. Change something. For example, mutate the drums on EUCLID with **OCT+**, or mute track 2 with **GLO** + the second
   black key. Then hold **SAVE** and press **D4**: **SAVED B**.
4. To hear them: **SAVE + F3** plays A and **SAVE + G3** plays B, each one from the next bar.
5. Stop with **PLAY**. Hold **SAVE** and press **G5**: **ARRANGE** opens and says **NO ROWS**.
6. Press **OCT+**: **ROW ADDED** (a row of part A, 4 bars long). **KNOB 3 BARS** sets how many bars it lasts. Press
   **OCT+** again (the row is copied) and set that row to part **B** with **KNOB 2 PART**. Repeat until you have, for
   example, A · B · A · B.
7. Press **PLAY**: the song plays from the top. **PLAY** again stops it, and the music you had before comes back
   (**SONG END**).
8. To choose how it ends, turn **KNOB 1** past the last row (**+ ADD**, then **END**) and pick **STOP**, **x2…x8** or
   **REPEAT** with **KNOB 2**.
9. Leave it stopped for a few seconds. Parts saved with SAVE + a key go to memory once you stop, and ARRANGE saves
   itself about 2 seconds after each change, while stopped.

**If it doesn't sound, or you're lost**

- **PART EMPTY**: a row points at a part you haven't saved.
- **ADD A SONG ROW**: ARRANGE has no rows yet.
- To delete a row: stopped, on ARRANGE, **KNOB 1** to the row, **OCT−** (**DELETE SONG ROW?**), then **OCT+**. It
  cannot be undone.

## 4. Let the machine write: GENERATOR, RADIO and MOOD

**What.** A whole piece made by the device in the genre you pick, and a radio that never ends.

**Why.** The generator knows each genre's rules (tempo, drums, chords, sounds) and builds a piece from a number, the
**seed**. The same settings always give the same piece.

> **First:** the generator replaces the sounds and steps of every track. **GLO + G4** gives you back what you had.
> What it makes is a **draft** (you'll see **DRAFT**): it is not saved by itself. Step 7 shows you how to keep it.

**How**

1. Press **HOME** and, still holding it, turn **SELECT** right away. If you hold HOME alone, the MENU opens. Or tap
   **HOME** until the **GENE** tab lights. Turn **SELECT** to **GENERATOR 1**.
2. **KNOB 1 GENRE**: **EXPLORE** (no genre, anything goes), **RANDOM**, or a genre (HOUSE, CUMBIA, HIP HOP,
   DUBSTEP…).
3. Press **OCT+**. The piece appears with its name (for example **HOUSE 0421**). Press **PLAY**.
4. Not your thing? Press **OCT+** again for another seed and another piece. **KNOB 3 ENERGY** makes it calmer or more
   intense.
5. **SELECT** to **GENERATOR 2**: **DRUMS**, **BASS**, **CHORDS**, **ARP**. Lower one and press **OCT+**: you get the
   same piece with that one thing changed.
6. **GENERATOR 4**: **KNOB 3 RADIO ON**. While the draft plays, the generator makes the next piece by itself and
   brings it in on the bar line: in a loop, every 16, 8 or 4 bars depending on the tempo. **KNOB 4 MOOD** goes from
   **CALM** to **DANCE**. RADIO and MOOD are not saved with the project.
7. **To keep it**: the status line reads **DRAFT · SAVE KEEPS · OCT+ NEW**. Stop with **PLAY** and tap **SAVE**:
   **PROJECTS** opens with SAVE already picked. **Pick an empty project with KNOB 1**, because otherwise it saves over
   the project you had open. Check that **KNOB 4** says **OCT+**, press **OCT+**, press **OCT+** again on the NAME
   screen, and the screen says **SAVED SONG** and the number.

**If it doesn't sound, or you're lost**

- **SONG PLAYS: STOP IT TO EDIT**: a song is playing. Stop it, or hold **SAVE** and press **D5** so that PLAY plays
  the loop.
- **GLO + G4** brings back the music from before the generator. Your saved project is still in memory: to open it, go
  to **SAVE › PROJECTS**, pick it with **KNOB 1**, then **KNOB 2 LOAD** and **OCT+**.

## 5. Effects and punch-ins

**What.** Give your sounds space with reverb and echo, and "break" the loop live with the keys.

**Why.** The **reverb** (a room) and the **delay** (an echo) are shared by all the tracks. A track's **send** says how
much of it goes into each effect. A **punch-in** is an effect that lasts only while you hold its key.

**How: sends and effects**

1. With the music playing, pick a track with **ALGORITHM** (T2, say) and press **FX**: the **SENDS** page (the
   breadcrumb reads **FX · TRACK · SENDS**). **KNOB 3 REV** sends it to the reverb and **KNOB 2 DLY** to the delay.
   Every track has its own sends.
2. Tap **FX** twice: **DLY**. **KNOB 1 TIME** (the echo's time: 1/8, 1/4…), **KNOB 2 FEEDBK** (how many repeats) and
   **KNOB 4 MIX**.
3. Tap **FX** again: **REVERB**. **KNOB 1 TYPE** (**ROOM**, **SPRING**, **SHIMR**, **RESO**, **CLOUD**) and
   **KNOB 2 SIZE** (how long the tail is).

**How: punch-ins**

4. With the music playing, hold **FX**: the keyboard is drawn with the name of each effect.
5. Still holding FX, hold **F3** (**RPT 1/8**): the loop repeats in eighths. Let go and it comes back exactly as it
   was. Try **C4** (**FREEZE**), **A#4** (**TAPE STOP**) and **F4** (**1/2 TEMPO**).
6. The first six black keys mute **T1** to **T6** while you hold them. With FX held, the four knobs move the macros
   **M1–M4**.
7. To keep both hands free, hold **FX** until the map appears and let go without touching anything: it stays
   **[FX] LOCKED**. Press **FX** again to release it.

**If it doesn't sound, or you're lost**

- If you can't hear an effect, check that the track's send (**FX › SENDS**) isn't at 0.
- Too much effect? Lower **REV** and **DLY** on SENDS. Punch-ins end when you let go of the key.

## 6. Saving without losing anything

**What.** Know where everything lives, so you never lose your work.

**Why.** What plays is in working memory, and it is gone at power-off. Only what you saved stays.

| What | How it is saved |
| --- | --- |
| The music now | Not by itself. Save it into a part. |
| A part A–D | **SAVE + C4…F4** (quick, also while playing) or **SAVE › PART SLOTS** (with a name, stopped). |
| ARRANGE (the rows) | By itself, about 2 seconds after each change, while stopped. |
| A whole song into another project | **SAVE › PROJECTS**, **KNOB 4 SAVE** (stopped). |
| A single sound | **SAVE › USER** (32 slots). |
| The automatic copy | Only if you turn on **MENU › SYSTEM › RESTORE** (RESTORE LAST, OFF by default). It writes 10 s after the last change, while stopped, and never at power-off. |
| UNDO | One step only, and it is gone at power-off. |

**The draft (DRAFT).** After the **GENERATOR**, or after **SAVE › TOOLS › CLEAR PROJECT**, what plays is a draft:
nothing is written to memory (not the automatic copy, not ARRANGE), and a save on PART SLOTS says **PART STORED IN
DRAFT**. To keep it, save it whole from **SAVE › PROJECTS** with **KNOB 4 SAVE**. If you then want to keep working on
it like any other project, open it: **PROJECTS**, **KNOB 1** to the project, **KNOB 2 LOAD**, **OCT+**.

> ### ⚠ Before you save over a song
>
> - **SAVE + C4…F4** and **SAVE + OCT+** never ask: they replace that part at once (**SAVED A**).
> - **At power-on** the last project you used is open. A SAVE + C4 of new music writes over its part A. Open an empty
>   project first (tour 3, step 1).
> - On **PART SLOTS**, if the part already holds something, it asks **OVERWRITE PROJECT A?** (it says PROJECT plus
>   the part's letter). **OCT+** takes you to NAME, and you can still cancel there with **OCT−**. **OCT+** on NAME
>   writes.
> - On **PROJECTS**, if the project already holds something, it asks **OVERWRITE PROJECT 2?**. If you then confirm
>   with **OCT+** on NAME, it **replaces the whole project**: all four parts and their rows. A part your song doesn't
>   have ends up empty in the destination. With **SCOPE LOOP** (KNOB 3 on NAME) only the loop is saved, and B–D and
>   the rows are left empty.
> - After the **GENERATOR**, SAVE offers the project you had open. Pick another one with **KNOB 1**.
> - Holding **SAVE** and turning **KNOB 1** switches project and loads its part A: unsaved music is lost, and UNDO
>   can't bring it back.
> - **UNDO does not undo a save.**
> - If you saved with SAVE + a key while playing, **stop before you power off**: only then does it reach memory.
> - To keep everything safe, make a complete backup with the web editor (**Save backup**). See
>   [the manual, § 15](MANUAL.md#15-web-tools-emulator-installer-editor-backup).

**If you're lost:** **STOP TO SAVE** means you have to stop first (**PLAY**). The whole chapter is
[the manual, § 12](MANUAL.md#12-saving-and-loading).
