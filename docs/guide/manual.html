JIANT FM — User Manual

JIANT FM V10.8.21 for the M-VAVE FM-1

Versión en español: MANUAL.es.md.

This manual is for musicians who have just installed JIANT FM and want to know how to play it, build songs with it and, above all, save their work without losing anything. It follows the firmware as it is in that version: every button, knob and message is named exactly as the device shows it.

If you only read one chapter, read 12. Saving and loading. Coming from V10.6c (the last public version) or earlier: What's new in this version.

Essential commands

Navigate

HOME EDIT SEQ FX GLO ENV LFO SCL ARP SAVE
From elsewhere: the page you were last on in that section. Already inside: the first page of the next group.
SELECT
Walks every page of the section, a page a detent.
ALGORITHM
Picks the track (T1–T4, or T1–T6 with TRACKS 6).
PRESETS
Always presets: the track's sounds. SEQ: the patterns. On SEQ › STEP FX it picks the step.
KNOB 1–4
The four cards on screen. KNOB 1 picks (step, bar, slot, row).
HOME held
The MENU.

Play and record

PLAY
Start / stop. Held about 0.7 s: PANIC, all notes off.
GLO + PLAY
Restart from the top.
REC
Arms live recording on the selected track. Held alone and let go: UNDO (again: redo).
OCT− OCT+
Keyboard octave; both together: back to 0. On action pages and dialogs: back / confirm.
GLO + SELECT
Tempo, from any page.

Hold a button (layers and combos)

FX held
Punch-in effects on the keys.
LFO held
MACRO layer: KNOB 1–4 are M1–M4.
SCL held
The keys set the root.
HOME + SELECT
The GENERATOR.
EDIT + SELECT
The sound dice (OCT+ keeps, OCT− cancels).
EDIT + PRESETS
The first sound of the next / previous engine.
GLO + G4
UNDO the last big change.
SEQ + G5
Clear the cursor step.
SAVE + C5
NAME: rename.
SEQ + REC
Clear all sequences (asks first).

What's new in this version

New in V10.8.21 (since V10.7.81)

The betas V10.8.1 to V10.8.20 in one list. Where a chapter below still describes the older screen, this list wins.

  • Song mode (HOME › JAM · TAKE · ARRANGE): play the four sequences A–D of every track live (F3–B3 pick A–D on all tracks, OCT+ GO, OCT− STOP, CAMBIO sets when a change lands). REC records the take; K4 LOCK plays it back and what you play over it is marked YOU; with a track selected, REC redoes that track for one round and OCT− undoes it. TAKE edits the changes (K1 pick, K2 move, K3 sequence, K4 bars, OCT+ new, OCT− delete). A help line on JAM and TAKE says what to do next (MENU › DISPLAY › HELP OFF hides it). GLO › LIVE is gone: JAM plays the sequences live.
  • Step components you play (SEQ › STEP FX): the PRESETS knob picks the step; the 16 white keys play components while held — STUTTER, STUTTER /2, /4, RATCHET x2 x3 x4 x8, ARP UP / DOWN / UP-DOWN, OCTAVE UP / DOWN, RANDOM, TRANSPOSE +2, CHORD, MUTE — and with REC they are written into the step with their value and length (into COMP, or COMP 2 when COMP is taken). A big banner names the key and what it does while held (also on the FX layer's punch-ins).
  • DRONE CLAY (SEQ › CLAY, from JIANT BYTE): a sequencer per track — a very low drone in the track's own voice (RATE re-strikes it with the transport, or holds it) and four modulators (shape, euclid, amount, decay, gestures) aimed at any of the track's parameters; note gestures (ARP, CHORD, HIT) play on the scale. On MONO / LEGATO it yields the voice to the steps; a TIME or PITCH punch-in restarts it from its first hit.
  • Which track you are on, always: a bar on top reads TRACK N / ENGINE in the track's own colour (T1 yellow, T2 orange, T3 red, T4 magenta, T5 violet, T6 blue); the same colours on the mixer, the beings, JAM and TAKE.
  • Macros as destinations: LFO › MACROS — KNOB 1 picks the macro, its destinations add up (16 routes in all); in the MOD matrix M1–M4 are destinations, not sources.
  • Redesigned screens: FX in the HUD look (DELAY's echoes a tunnel, REVERB cells, CHORUS L/R orbit, SLICER, MASTER, DIST); FX › SENDS as columns; GLO › MIXER channels; ENV as layers of the last notes; LFO as a satellite writing its wave; EDIT › FOCUS a horizontal heat bar with the parameter's whole name and its modulation; ARRANGE and GENERATOR a living score (sections as wide as their bars, one lane per track); SCL › KEY the scale's whole name, its notes and degrees; SCL › CHORD the chord really played (D MIN7…), its notes and intervals, the seven degrees' chords. Names are whole where they fit (ECOSYSTEM, GENERATOR, MIXER, MODULATION…).
  • Power-on: the boot "Six threads" with the signature drawn smooth and the version shown; the click at power-on is gone (the output opens after the audio has started, then fades in).
  • Removed for room: GLO › LIVE, SEQ › STRETCH, MUTATE, the sequence dice (SEQ + SELECT; the sound dice stays), GLO › GLOBAL COMP, ENV › TRACKS and the one-time teaching hints. Old projects open: their settings are kept and ignored.

New in V10.7.81 (since V10.7.16)

The betas V10.7.17 to V10.7.80 in one list. Where a chapter below still describes the older screen, this list wins.

Drums - DRUM-X in 8 pages: KIT (MORPH SNAP TONE DECAY), KIT 2 (NOISE BODY GLUE GAIN), SOUND (PITCH DECAY LEVEL), SOUND 2 (NOISE COLOR PMOD), SOUND 3 (WAVE FILTER P.ENV), SOUND 4 (SIDE SEND KEY MAP), CROSS MOD, FILTER. RATIO (¼ … 16, 24 values) on KNOB 4, MOD AMT in COLOR's place. Two kits are made of formulas, GLITCH FM and MATH LAB; EDIT + SELECT (DICE) gives about half the sounds a formula of their own.

Engines - MATH MORPH: ALGO B (where CHIP was) and MORPH A → B (where BEND was) glide from one formula to another. - Every engine like the kit: FILTER (TYPE CUT RES IMAGE), PLAY (POLY GLIDE UNISON VEL), GAIN 0 … +12 dB clean. - EDIT's pages by their names, the extras on one MORE, now before PLAY; FOCUS drawn in heat colours.

Harmony and sequences - SCL › HARMONY, its own tab (KEY CHORD HARMONY SCENE): PROG LENGTH EVOLVE SKIP; every melodic sequence follows the progression, shown as roman numerals in the key, the playing chord lit. Performance - Hold FX: a map of named cells (the effects by name, M1–M4 on top, the mutes T1–T6 below). - Punch-in editor: OCT+ on FX › PUNCH IN (or FX + OCT+). Each key: WHAT (an effect, or a parameter: M1–M4, CUT, PITCH, TIMBRE, VOLUME, PAN, DIST, CHORUS, DELAY, REVERB, VIBRATO), HOW (HOLD LATCH QUANT, or RAMP UP / DN, SINE, STUTTER, S&H), AMOUNT (−100 … +100 %) and TIME (1/16 … 8 bars); two slots, A and B. New effects: REVERSE, FREEZE, TAPE STOP, PITCH UP / DOWN, STRETCH. FX › PUNCH IN's knobs are the macros M1–M4. - LIFE (HOME › LIFE): the tracks move each other (FILTER TIMBRE PUMP FX). - SCENE plays on the live tracks only; its LEADER can be the keys or a track's notes.

Generator (GENE) - Seeds are fused (A+B in the name), never the same throw twice; it waits for the bar while playing; GENE 4: KEY NEW / KEEP. - DUBSTEP (the wobble, its rate moving; MATH basses), SHOEGAZE, METALCORE, CUMBIA villera, eight more progressions (neo-soul, jazz, IDM, progressive); each genre its FX STYLE; MATH, WEST and STRING more often.

Screens and settings - The song's pages live in HOME › SONG: TIMELINE ARRANGE PARTS CLIPS (SAVE saves and loads). - MENU › DISPLAY › PRESETS: SCROLL (the knob walks the sounds) or MENU (the list opens as you turn). - MENU › HELP: a knob's meaning in the header where it is not obvious. Oscilloscopes instead of decorative bars. - Defaults: LARGE off, CLICK on REC, COUNT-IN 1 BAR. - GLO held + G4: UNDO.

The beta V10.8.1 redesigns the song mode (each track its sequences A–D, CAMBIO, a JAM take with LOCK); it is not in this version. See the README.

Everything since V10.6c, the last public version, in one list; each item links to its place in the manual.

Before you update - 6 songs, not 8 (V10.6p). Songs 7 and 8 no longer open: their flash is kept for a drum sample bank. SCL > SCENE's DRUM role can take a kit generated for the genre (SCENE DRUM GEN). - The thermal palette everywhere: every note its own heat, the piano roll coloured by pitch class (V10.6w).

0. Quick start

Install

  1. Open the web installer in Chrome or Edge on a computer, or Chrome on Android with a USB OTG cable (iPhone and iPad have no Web MIDI): https://juanjiant-bit.github.io/JIANT/webapp/installer/
  2. Connect the FM-1 directly with a USB data cable.
  3. Coming from another custom firmware (Felucca, Skiff, baudgirl or any other)? Return to official first, with the installer's Return to official button (it also saves that firmware's backup). Writing JIANT straight over another custom firmware has left an FM-1 unable to start.
  4. Press Install. It first checks the FM-1's firmware and writes only over official V16, official V15 or JIANT (anything else: it sends you to Return to official first). Don't unplug the cable while it writes.

Return to official takes M-VAVE's own FM-1.fwsc of V16 (718,196 bytes, what M-VAVE offers now) or V15 (699,956 bytes), unzipped from M-VAVE's download page and checked byte for byte. It saves a complete JIANT backup first; to come back, press Install and restore the backup with the editor.

JIANT has no user samples: if you come from Felucca, its user samples (USR1–3) are lost on install. Keep your WAV files.

Your first sound (1 minute)

  1. Power on. The signature draws itself, then you land on HOME: six creatures, one per track. (V10.6e) The tracks start as a band in tune: T1 DRUM-X (ACOUSTIC), T2 ANALOG (PLUCK), T3 PHASE (INIT SAW), T4 FM6 (FM BASS), T5 VOICE (CHANT) and T6 ANIMAL (WHISTLE), each its preset's sound, dry.
  2. Turn ALGORITHM to pick a track (T1–T6). With the default TRACKS 4 it stops at T4: T5 and T6 appear only with TRACKS 6 (see 13).
  3. Turn PRESETS to browse sounds. Play the keys.
  4. Turn KNOB 1–4: on HOME they are the four most useful controls of the track's engine.
  5. Don't like it? Hold EDIT and turn SELECT: the sound is rolled into a new variation and you hear it at once. OCT+ OK keeps it, OCT− CANCEL brings the sound back.

Your first song (5 minutes)

  1. Hold HOME and turn SELECT (or on HOME tap HOME twice: ECO → BEING → GEN). The GENERATOR opens on EXPLORE (no genre, every rule broken): press OCT+ as it is, or pick RANDOM or one of the genres with KNOB 1 or PRESETS first. A whole song idea appears: engines, sounds, parts, scale, tempo and effects. Press PLAY.
  2. Not your thing? Press OCT+ again: a new seed, a new piece. Too busy? Turn SELECT one detent right for GEN 2 and lower DRUMS or ARP, then OCT+: the same piece with that changed. OCT− goes back to ECO. To go back to what was there before: hold GLO and press G4 (UNDO).
  3. Like it? Stop (PLAY), then press SAVE (it opens TIMELINE, the song) and turn SELECT three detents right (past PARTS and CLIPS): SECTIONS, the project's four sections. Turn KNOB 4 to the right so its card says OCT+, then press OCT+. Type a name with the keys (or keep the one offered) and press OCT+ again. The screen says SAVED.
  4. Make a variation (change some steps, mute a track) and save it into section B: on SECTIONS turn KNOB 1 to B, then KNOB 4 and OCT+ as before.
  5. Turn SELECT two detents right (past PROJECTS): ARRANGE (the song's rows). Press OCT+ to add a row, set its section with KNOB 2 and its length with KNOB 3 (bars). Add a row for A, one for B. Press PLAY: the song plays the rows in order.

That's the whole loop: make music → save it into a section → chain sections in ARRANGE.

1. The panel

The names below are the ones the firmware uses (the HARDWARE CALIBRATION screen asks for them by these names).

Encoders and knobs

Control What it does
SELECT (encoder) Alone (0.9.2): walks the section's pages, one page a detent, as one chain, stopping at its first and last page (no levels, no zoom; see How navigation works). In the MENU: its rows, all four tabs in one chain. With a button held: HOME + SELECT GEN, EDIT / SEQ / LFO / FX + SELECT that part's dice, GLO + SELECT the tempo, ENV / SCL / ARP / SAVE + SELECT walk that section's chain from wherever you are.
ALGORITHM (encoder) Selects the track from any page: T1–T6 (T1–T4 with TRACKS 4). The track is always ALGORITHM's (0.9.2: PRESETS no longer picks it on ENV, LFO, CHORD, ARP). In the MENU: the next / previous tab. With a layer held: its view, MAP ↔ KNOBS ↔ STATE.
PRESETS (encoder) Always presets (0.9.2): the selected track's sounds (every engine's factory presets, then your user presets) on every page; on the SEQ pages the patterns (it opens the PHRASES loader with the next pattern picked: OCT+ loads it, OCT− back to the SEQ page); on GEN the GENRE. In the MENU and with a button held it does nothing, except EDIT held (V10.7): it jumps to the first sound of the next / previous engine, the engine's name on screen. It never changes page, track, module or row.
KNOB 1–4 The four cards (columns) on screen. A page that picks something picks it with KNOB 1 (the step, the bar, the slot, the stage, the operator, the kit's sound, the row). With a layer held, the layer's own knobs. Turning fast sweeps the whole range, slowly fine-tunes (MENU › CONTROL › KNOB ACCEL turns this off). In the MENU: KNOB n the setting on card n.
MASTER The output volume.

Key velocity and aftertouch (V10.6z7)

The FM-1's keys F3..C5 sit over a carbon pill whose resistance falls as you press harder (the method is Charles Vestal's, Velo, github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-velocity, GPL-3.0). JIANT reads it on every strike:

  • Velocity: how hard the key lands, in the first milliseconds after it touches. It is continuous (1..127: every strike between soft and hard gives a value between), then MENU > CONTROL > VELOCITY's curve and VEL MIN's floor. On a synth track a note waits for its strike at most 2.2 ms (V10.7.10: a hard limit, even when a reading of the key is lost), under what you can feel; on a DRUM track (the selected one, or KEY SPLIT's half that goes to one) it never waits: it plays at once with the strike as read so far, or the key's recent average. It reaches everything a key plays: the sound (the engines and the matrix's VEL), chords and strums, the arpeggiator, recording (only 127 records as an accent) and MIDI OUT. VELOCITY OFF: every key 100, as before.
  • The top row (C#5..G5) has no sensor (the FM-1 cannot read those seven keys' pressure), so it plays the average of your recent strikes (100 until you play the others). It has no aftertouch (a chord with sensed keys keeps theirs).
  • Calibration: every key is different, and each learns your own soft, normal and hard strikes as you play (VEL LEARN ON, the default: it moves slowly, a stray strike does not throw it). It starts from the factory calibration (measured on the owner's FM-1) and is kept in the settings, written half a minute after you stop playing. VEL LEARN RESET puts the factory calibration back. To see it at work: the page https://juanjiant-bit.github.io/JIANT/webapp/vel/ (En vivo, Chrome, over the editor's USB link) shows each note's velocity and each key's calibration.
  • Aftertouch: with a key held, press it further down: its pressure (0..127) is the matrix's AT source (EDIT > MOD: any destination: filter, vibrato depth, level ...). New sounds (INIT SOUND, a new project) route it to the filter (slot 4, AT -> CUT +20) when that slot is free; saved projects and presets keep their own matrix. A resting finger does nothing (a dead zone), the value glides (no zipper), and lifting the key ends it. MENU > CONTROL > AFTERTOUCH sets how sensitive it is (LOW, MID, HIGH) or turns it OFF. It works with OCT− / OCT+ held too; it is recorded as automation with the expression keys and sent as channel aftertouch with MIDI OUT ALL.
  • A sound's VEL (EDIT > VOICE 2, KNOB 3; 0..100 %, every engine but DRUM): how much velocity reaches this sound. 100 % (every project, preset and song made before it) plays each note as hard as it was played; less brings every note's velocity toward 100 (50 %: a strike of 60 plays 80, one of 120 plays 110); 0 % plays every note at 100, as VELOCITY OFF does, but for this track only. It applies to every source: the keys, the sequencer's steps, the arp, the punch-in repeats and MIDI in; the matrix's VEL reads the same value. A step's ACCENT still plays 127, and HUMAN and a RATCHET's DECAY still vary the notes. Recording keeps the velocity you played, so turning VEL up later brings the dynamics back. The factory bass presets load at 0 % (a bass is better even), GENE's bass at 75 % (its composed ghost notes still softer); every other preset at 100 %. Kept with the sound (projects, user presets, songs); NRPN 80, and the web editor's VOICE group.

How navigation works: six rules (0.9.2)

Since 0.9.2 every section is driven the same way (the design the owner approved, the mock-up "navegación"): learn it once and every section is known. Until 0.9.1 each part was a lens of three levels with sets of knobs; the pages, their knobs, their pictures and every function are the same, only the way between them changed.

  1. SELECT walks the pages. Turning SELECT goes through all the pages of the section, in one chain, a page a detent, and stops at the first and the last. No levels, no zoom. FX: SENDS → DIST → AUTO PAN → BUSES → DLY → DLY 2 → DLY 3 → REVERB → … → PUNCH.
  2. The section's button: in, or on. From another section it brings you back to the page you were last on there. Already inside, it jumps to the first page of the next group (and goes round). On FX › DLY 2, FX goes to REVERB; on EDIT › FILTER, EDIT goes to FOCUS.
  3. PRESETS loads presets. Always: the track's sounds in every section, the patterns in SEQ (the PHRASES loader), the genre in GEN. It never changes page, track or module (in the MENU it does nothing).
  4. KNOB 1 picks. When a page picks something (a step, a bar, a slot, a stage, an operator, the kit's sound, a row) KNOB 1 is the one that picks it. The track is always ALGORITHM's. SEQ › STEP: KNOB 1 walks all 64 steps, bar after bar; the new SEQ › BAR page: KNOB 1 the bar.
  5. Hold = the layer. Holding a button opens its layer of shortcuts, as before (past the HOLD time, MENU > HOLD, 0.4 s by default: the keys and knobs change function while you hold it, a map shows what each key does; a key or a knob opens it at once; let go and you are back). Held + SELECT: HOME GEN, EDIT / SEQ / LFO / FX their dice, GLO the tempo, ENV / SCL / ARP / SAVE walk their chain. (Held + PRESETS no longer walks pages: rule 3; V10.7: EDIT held + PRESETS jumps engine to engine.)
  6. The screen says where you are. On top, the tabs are the section's groups, the one you are on lit and a dot for each of its pages. At the foot: SECTION · GROUP · page and n/N, the page's place in the chain: FX · DELAY · DLY 2 6/16. The same in AIR, COLORS, BOTTOM and HUD, every palette.
  • Each section remembers the page it was left on (EDIT one for the synths and one each for FM6 and DRUM); at power-on SEQ opens on STEP, ENV on ENV, LFO on LFO, the others on their first page.
  • SCL and ARP are two sections (until 0.9.1 one lens): SCL is KEY · CHORD, ARP is ARP · ARP 2 (one group: an ARP tap inside goes back to ARP).
  • GEN is three HOME pages (GEN 1–3, HOME's last group), no longer a screen of its own: the section buttons work there, OCT+ makes the piece, OCT− goes back to ECO. HOME + SELECT still opens it from anywhere.
  • SAVE from outside comes back to the SAVE page you left; USER and SECTIONS come with their SAVE already picked, so SAVE, OCT+, OCT+ still stores the sound (or the section).
  • EDIT is always EDIT: clearing a step went to the SEQ layer (SEQ + G5, CLR STEP; on AUTO LIST it deletes the record; TIME turned to REST clears a note too) and renaming to the SAVE layer (SAVE + C5, NAME).
  • The MENU (hold HOME): SELECT walks every row (DISPLAY → CONTROL → AUDIO → SYSTEM), ALGORITHM jumps tabs, a knob changes the value, HOME closes it.
  • Some pages only appear when they apply: FM6's operator pages on an FM6 track, DRUM-X's pages on a DRUM track, RETRIG on MATH, SWARM's pages on an EXT track playing SWARM, and with LOAD ECO the SLICER and CHORUS pages are hidden; the chain skips them.

4. Engines

Twelve engines, all synthesis, no samples: ANALOG, FM6, PHASE, MATH (LOFI until V10.6e), VOICE, WHEEL, NOISE, DRUM-X, ANIMAL, STRING (a plucked, struck or bowed string, and guitar feedback), ADD (additive) and EXT (external signals: the USB audio input, or SWARM, the Bluetooth around it). Any engine can go on any track. Pick them with PRESETS (one list over every engine) or with KNOB 2 (ENG) on SAVE > PRESETS. Each track's four HOME knobs are listed too.

The order (V10.6e): PRESETS, KNOB 2 (ENG) and the web editor's lists show the engines as DRUM-X, ANALOG, PHASE, FM6, VOICE, ANIMAL (the tracks at power-on, T1–T6), then MATH, WHEEL, NOISE, STRING, ADD, EXT. Only the order you see changed: projects, user presets, favourites and backups name engines by their numbers, which stay, so everything saved before loads as it was. PROGRAM CHANGE keeps 0.9.8's numbers (see 14).

Smooth changes: switching a track to another engine fades the old one out (about 12 ms) and what you are holding (keys, or the notes the sequencer holds) goes on in the new engine, fading in; nothing is cut. Changing a list value (WAVE, CHIP, a type) while notes sound makes a short dip (about 3 ms) instead of a click; the notes go on.

  • PHASE: phase distortion. WAVE / WAVE2: SAW, SQR, PLS, DSIN, SPLS, RSAW, RTRI, RTRP. LINE: MIX or RING. FB feeds the output back into the phase.
  • MATH (LOFI until V10.6e, its formulas alone): WAVE BYTE or FLOAT; CHIP 4BIT, 4B/2, 8BIT (FLOAT: FULL), 1BIT. (The rest of this entry is from LOFI's time: BYTE and FLOAT work as described.)
  • BYTE (bytebeat): KNOB 3 becomes ALGO (32 formulas), KNOB 4 VAR (mutates the formula); EDIT 2 becomes MOTION, GRIT, BEND, LOOP. MOTION speeds the formula's rhythm up or down (64 = as written), GRIT is a rate crusher, BEND folds time, LOOP repeats a tiny stretch (high LOOP turns the noise into a tuned note).
  • FLOAT (floatbeat): CHIP becomes the resolution (4BIT, 4B/2, FULL, 1BIT), KNOB 3 ALGO (32 formulas made of sines), KNOB 4 VAR; EDIT 2: MOTION, FEEDBK, BEND, LOOP. Hold a key: the formula plays in that key.

On ANALOG the SHAPE modulation moves the morph too, so an LFO or envelope sweeps the wave. The page draws one cycle of the morphed wave and says which two waves it blends. KNOB 2 is DRIFT (0–100 %, 0 = off): each voice's pitch (up to about ±27 cents) and filter wander slowly, each in its own way, as old analog oscillators do. A chord comes alive; at high values it goes seasick.
  • VOICE: a formant voice (after klattsch): vowels, talk, breath. Since 0.8.5 it also sings words and lyrics in Spanish, English and Japanese: see VOICE: words and lyrics.
  • WHEEL: a drawbar organ: registrations (REG), percussion (PERC: OFF, 2ND, 3RD, 2SOFT, 3SOFT, 2SLOW, 3SLOW), key click, drive, ROTOR (OFF, SLOW, FAST). EDIT > ROTOR (WHEEL only): DEPTH (how much the rotary speaker swirls: 100 % as before, 0 % a still speaker) and DUST (0–100 %: an old organ's crackle and hiss while it plays).
  • NOISE: coloured and metallic noise. MODE: ANLG, DUST, LFSR, META (DUST is only the crackle: at DENS 0 it still crackles softly). PEAK is its own resonance.
  • DRUM: DRUM-X, see 5.
  • ANIMAL: animal calls from physical models, see ANIMAL below.

ANIMAL (0.8.3)

Animal calls built the way animals make them: a source that oscillates or strikes (a bird's syrinx, a cricket's file and scraper, a cicada's tymbal, a frog's larynx, a wing) exciting one or two resonators (the wing, the abdomen, the vocal sac, the vocal tract), driven by a slow layer of pressure, tension and rhythm. MODE picks the source; every mode reads the eight knobs its own way and the screen relabels them. The key sets the tension (the pitch, the carrier), the velocity the pressure; a note starts a call, so a sequence of notes is a sequence of calls, in tune with the rest of the music.

The call's rhythm (every mode but CMOS): RATE is the syllable rate (0.05–40 a second); GROUP how they group: HOLD (one syllable as long as the note: a coo, a howl, a drone), TRILL (no gaps) or n+m (n syllables, then m silent ones: 3+9 is a field cricket's chirp of three); TEMP (°C, 22 as written) scales every rate the way temperature does in insects and frogs (Dolbear's law: a cricket at 30 °C chirps about 2.4 times as fast as at 14 °C). SIZE moves the whole animal up or down from the key, a semitone a step (64: as played, lower: smaller and higher; GLOTT: the vocal tract's length instead, every formant together, the pitch left alone). SWEEP is each syllable's pitch gesture: an exponential hook from below (+) or above (−) onto the note, up to two octaves; above 16 syllables a second it narrows, as a bird's trill can only be that fast and that wide (Podos).

  • CMOS: the owner's breadboard circuit, a 40106 / 4093 Schmitt-trigger chip, two big capacitors, three pots and a probe touched by hand ("a machine of bird, monkey and bug songs"). A fast RC oscillator (an octave above the key: C5 chirps at ~1.05 kHz, as in the video) charges and discharges its capacitor exponentially between two thresholds; its square wave is band-limited at the exact edge. A slow one gates it (the 4093's NAND) and its capacitor's voltage sweeps it (the 40106's FM): the exponential charge is the "hook" you hear at the start of each chirp. RATE the slow one (the chirps a second), CAP how long it charges against how long it discharges (short blips .. long sweeps), HYST its threshold window (the hook's depth; + rises onto the note, − falls onto it), FM 0 the gate alone (beeps), 20 gate and sweep (the bird), 40 the sweep alone (a siren), TOUCH the hand on the probe (faster, less regular), COUPLE a second fast oscillator coupled both ways (locking, then chaos: bugs), GROUP a third, very slow one that groups the chirps into phrases, speeding up as it goes (monkeys).
  • PULSE: an escapement. Each tooth of a cricket's file (each rib of a cicada's tymbal, each pulse of a frog's larynx) is an impulse into a resonator tuned to the key (the wing, the abdomen, the vocal sac). CLICK 100 %: one pulse a cycle of the resonance, the clockwork cricket's pure tone; lower, one pulse every 2 .. 64 cycles: clicks ringing it (a cicada's buzz, a frog's croak; at 1/2 a treefrog's two peaks an octave apart). Q: broadband (a katydid's rasp) to pure (a cricket). DETUNE: the pulses off the resonance (beats, an "anomalous" song) and jittered.
  • WHSTL: a syrinx as a Hopf oscillator: the tone is born when the air-sac PRESSure crosses a threshold and grows with it (its level ~ the square root of how far past: below the threshold it is silent, whatever the envelope), so every syllable has a real onset; the pitch rises a little with the level. CHAOS: noise in the pressure and the pitch, and a second harmonic.
  • WING: a band-limited pulse at the wingbeat with a slow random DRIFT of pitch; BUZZ narrows it (brighter); each syllable is a fly-by: louder and brighter as it comes, its pitch shifted by DOPPLR (higher coming, lower going). HOLD: a steady hover.
  • GLOTT: a glottal pulse into two formants. MOUTH closed (oo) to open (aa), and darker to brighter; ROUGH in two halves of what mammals do: period doubling (a subharmonic, F0/2) up to 50 %, then a logistic map jittering each cycle (deterministic chaos: a roar); AIR: breath noise. A purr: play low (C1–C2), ROUGH halves the pitch.

Presets (24): CMOS BIRD, CMOS TRILL, CMOS MONKEY, CMOS BUG · CRICKET, TREE CRICKT, KATYDID, CICADA, TREEFROG, TOAD, BULLFROG · CANARY, CARDINAL, DOVE, OWL, PEEPER, BAT · MOSQUITO, BEE, FLY · WOLF, DOG BARK, ROAR, PURR. They are set to sound right around C5 (the frogs' and mammals' an octave or two lower: BULLFROG and OWL C4, WOLF G4, ROAR C3, PURR C2).

VOICE: words and lyrics (0.8.5)

VOICE sings: syllables, words and whole lyrics, in Spanish, English and Japanese. Turn it on with EDIT > WORDS > LANG (OFF, ES, EN, JA), or pick a word preset: CANTOR ES, CANTORA ES, SINGER EN, UTA JA, WORD CHOIR, SCAT EN, ROBOT EN, WHISPER JA. LANG OFF is the VOICE you know (its vowels), note for note as before.

How it sings. Every note sings the next syllable of the lyric. Hold the note and the vowel holds; a TIE holds it on; at the release the syllable's last consonants sound (the s of "es", the n of "sun"). Legato notes glide from syllable to syllable, and a syllable whose end was not sung yet gets it quickly before the next one. With several voices every voice sings the same syllable: a choir. The lyric loops when it ends.

How it sounds (0.9.8). Like a singer: the hiss of s, sh, f comes in and goes softly (band-passed, ~6 kHz for s, under the vowel's level), the formants glide from sound to sound without corners, a note's vowel carries into the next syllable, held notes take a gentle vibrato after about 0.3 s (each voice of a choir its own), and the voice is never perfectly still (a little jitter and shimmer). A glide between notes eases out of the first and into the second (an S-curve, as long as GLIDE says); singing words, legato notes glide a little (~60 ms) even with GLIDE at 0.

EDIT > WORDS (VOICE tracks only): LANG, LYRIC, POS; OCT+ (TYPE) opens the lyric's typing screen.

Writing a lyric. On the device: EDIT > WORDS, OCT+. It is the NAME screen with room for 63 characters (the field scrolls with the cursor): the keys type phone-style, KNOB 3 throws the dice (a phrase of the generator in the track's language and theme, to edit as you like), OCT+ sets it (also while playing), OCT− cancels. From the computer: the web editor's Sound tab shows a Lyric box on a VOICE track (LANG, LYRIC and the text; it turns accents and ñ into what the device reads). Each track has its own lyric, saved with the project section.

Text rules. - Spanish is read by rules, the way it is written: seseo (z and ce ci as s), ll and y as y, rr and an initial r trilled, j and ge gi as the jota, ch, qu, gu; diphthongs as one syllable (bien, cuidado). No accents needed; write ñ as NY (NINYO). - Japanese in romaji (Hepburn or kunrei), one note a mora: sakura 3, konnichiwa 5 (ko-n-ni-chi-wa), arigatou 5 (a long ou sung o-o); a doubled consonant (kitto) is a short stop, not a note. - English comes from a dictionary of ~2100 common words (from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary) with their syllables; a word it lacks is read by rough letter rules, which work less well. - A - on its own holds the vowel one more note (a melisma: A - MOR is a, a, mor). _ joins two words into one (MI_AMOR: mia-mor, the Spanish sinalefa). Punctuation and digits only separate words.

The screen. A singing VOICE's EDIT pages show the language and the source, the syllables sung, the word in large, its syllable's phonemes (the one sounding lit), your text from there on, and a vowel chart whose dot moves with the formants (F1 down, F2 across: where the tongue is), with the voicing and noise levels.

What to expect. This is formant synthesis, like a vintage speech chip that sings: vowels, s sh f, t k p, ch, m n l and the glides come through in context; b d g, v z and the English r are approximations, and intelligibility drops at very short notes (a syllable's consonants take 40–100 ms before its vowel). Spanish and Japanese read best (their spelling is regular); English depends on the dictionary.

Cost. No state per voice (the articulation is the track's); ~1 KB of RAM for the six lyrics and singers; ~42 KB of flash (the English dictionary is most of it); about +9 % CPU over VOICE's vowels, so four voices singing still cost well under ANALOG's eight.

Three engines draw their own creature (0.9.4)

Where every engine shows its being (EDIT › SOUND on the left, HOME › BEING large), three engines draw something of their own, in the same thermal line art (the colder the line, the less dense or quieter; white is hottest), moving only while the track sounds and resting in silence:

  • VOICE: the vocal tract, alive (0.9.9), seen close, the mouth and the neck filling the panel: the head's outline is a faint ghost (the brow, the nose, the lips, the chin, the throat) and the vocal tract a glowing tube of two walls, from the lips over the palate and the velum down the pharynx to the glottis, the trachea under it. It sings what you hear: the tongue (the tube's floor) rises and falls with the vowel's F1 and moves forward and back with F2, the jaw opens downward with F1, the lips round for o, u, w and sh and close for p, b, m, the lower lip touches the teeth for f and v, the tongue's tip, blade or body goes to the place of each consonant (t d n l r at the ridge, k g ng on the velum ..) and the tube narrows and heats there, with a hot mark where it closes; for m, n and ng the velum drops and a branch to the nose opens. Each glottal pulse is a bulge travelling up the tube (through the nose for m, n, ng), the vocal folds open and shut as a V with the voicing, and sound waves leave the mouth (the nose for the nasals); a jet of turbulence comes out of the constriction for s, sh, f, x and the bursts, and breath rises at the glottis for h and BREATH. The head nods a little at each vowel's onset and sways and breathes slowly with the level. With WORDS on it follows the syllable being sung (the phoneme, its formants and gains); without, VOWEL → VOWEL2 over TALK and BREATH (the nod at each note).
  • ANIMAL: a creature between a bird, an insect and a mammal. Its outline, legs, wing, antennae and eye are the same points in the three animals, mixed: MODE sets the animal (CMOS and WHSTL a bird, PULSE and WING an insect, GLOTT a mammal) and the creature metamorphoses to it in about half a second; SIZE leans it along insect – bird – mammal (well past the presets' sizes: PULSE turned up grows into a bird, GLOTT turned down into a bat-like bird), and CHAOS stirs it. With each call: the beak or jaw opens and the throat (syrinx, larynx) swells, song arcs leave the mouth, an insect's wings buzz (PULSE: stridulating, its hind leg rubbing; WING: flapping in a blur) and its abdomen's segments pulse, a mammal's ribs breathe.
  • DRUM-X: the kit as little beings ("BICHOS"), on EDIT › KIT (the first KIT page: the sound last struck named at the top right), on EDIT › SOUND 1–4 (the sound being edited lit and named) and on HOME › BEING; KIT 2, KIT 3 and X-MOD keep the colony (a being per sound in two rows). Each drum is a small being with eyes and a mouth, standing as a kit does: the kick large in the middle (its core hot orange), the snare flat on its stand, the toms on little legs (the floor tom on longer ones), the hi-hat two shells on a stand (together on CH, the top one lifting with its mouth open on OH), the crash on a long stem and the cowbell a small head over the kick (both BELL), the clap two little ones that clap together, the rim (RS) a spark on the snare's edge. On each hit the drum's being squashes, shuts its eyes and shouts (its mouth opens), as hard as the hit sounds (the lane's own envelope: the velocity, the accent) and springing back as the sound decays (DECAY); a GHOST squashes a little, a FLAM twice (its hit to come a shadow under the drum), a ratchet (x2 x4 x8) over and over.

They cost about what a being costs to draw (host: the EDIT panel ~113 µs against ~122 µs for ANALOG's being; the whole KIT screen with the kit's beings ~167 µs, as ANALOG's EDIT screen) and ~10 KB of flash for the three, 160 bytes of RAM; nothing in the sound.

Six more engines draw their own creature (0.9.6)

With 0.9.6 every engine has its own picture: ANALOG, FM6, WHEEL, NOISE, PHASE and MATH (LOFI then) draw theirs on EDIT › SOUND (on the left; the scope and the chips on the right as before; 0.9.7: with MENU › DISPLAY › SCOPE ON — OFF, the default, gives the creature the whole panel, centred, the sound's name a small caption in a corner) and on HOME › BEING (HOME's ecosystem keeps its beings), in the same thermal line art, the bichos' face shared with DRUM-X's beings, moving only while the track sounds:

  • ANALOG: a cell. Its membrane (three isotherms, the outer cold) is two cycles of the oscillators through the filter and the drive, drawn round in polar form: WAVE (and MORPH), DETUNE, MIX, NOISE and INT shape it, CUT on the note softens it, RES rings it; a SAW turns it into a pinwheel, a SQR into battlements. Where DRIVE clips the wave short spikes come out; a ring at CUT glows and spreads as much as RES; NOISE grows cilia; the two oscillators orbit inside at DETUNE's beat; the face shouts with the level and blinks on each note.
  • FM6: a colony of operators. The six operators are little beings wired as the algorithm (the 32 charts' cells and routes: carriers standing on the output's floor, modulators above, a pulse down each wire as fast as its modulator's RATIO). Each one is as big and hot as its LEVEL times its envelope now, has as many spikes as its RATIO, and the feedback operator curls a tail as long as FB. On OPERATOR and OPERATOR 2 the colony fills the panel with the operator picked lit (DETUNE makes it shimmer), "ALG 05 PAT" on the left and its frequency ("OP3 x1.50") on the right, the cards as before; on OP ENV the envelope as the patch has it (the stage KNOB 3 / 4 edit lit) and the operator's bicho rides it where its envelope is now: up the attack, along the hold, down the release.
  • WHEEL: nine tube worms, one per drawbar, as tall as its level (the registration's digits under them), each with a crown of as many tentacles as its harmonic; the rotor is a current that bends them (SLOW, FAST, its DEPTH), PERC makes its worm leap and shout, CLICK lets bubbles out of their mouths, DUST drifts motes.
  • NOISE: a swarm of protozoa round a queen: COLOR is how they walk (white: jittering in place; brown: drifting), FREQ how they swirl and how wide a ring they keep, PEAK how tight; in DUST each impulse makes some flare and jump; in LFSR and META they march in columns, one per bit of the register.
  • PHASE: a snail. The shell's chambers sit at the bent phase (even at DCW 0; as DCW rises they crowd where the phase rushes, which is what phase distortion does), coloured by the output; the foot's sole is the wave (WAVE and WAVE2 alternating); FB frays the spiral, SUB makes the outer whorl breathe, the eyestalks pull in on each note; with DETUNE or RING a little snail follows (RING: a thread between them).
  • MATH (LOFI): a caterpillar whose two skins are the last 256 samples out (the scope's own: nothing more is taken from the sound), held steps on BYTE, smooth on FLOAT. Its 8 segments are the byte's bits (the highest next to the head), each lit and lifting its legs with its bit; the antennae are tipped by the two lowest; the head is a bicho shouting with the level. On FLOAT and the chip waves (PLS TRI SAW NOIS WRAM) the same caterpillar, its skin the waveform and its segments lit by the wave's level.

Each draws into whatever rectangle it is given (EDIT's left panel, HOME's middle, the whole panel). About 14 KB of flash for the six, 1.4 KB of RAM (the pool); each page draws no dearer than ANALOG's EDIT page with its being did; nothing in the sound.

Since V10.6z6 SWARM is a melody, not only a drone: a lead voice plays notes on the tempo's grid, and how near something is decides what it plays and how it sounds. The devices heard around (MENU › SYSTEM › BLUETOOTH AIR) are still voices of their own underneath, a quieter bed.

The melody. On every step of the grid the lead looks at how the closeness changed since the last step: coming nearer, it goes up the scale; moving away, down; quick moves two degrees at once; still, MOVE decides (pedal, neighbours, the arpeggio). Its notes are in the track's ROOT / SCALE (SCL page), from C3 over two octaves (OCTAVE past half: three), turning back at the ends. With SCALE CHR it plays the blues (1 b3 3 4 b5 5 b7) and slides into b3, 3, b5, 5 and b7 from a semitone below now and then. The devices' own hits wait for the grid's next step too, so nothing comes in off the beat.

The devices. Every device heard adds modulation depth: one device mild, eight extreme. CHAOS is the ceiling: what acts is CHAOS × that depth (the chain of voices, the fold, the ring as before), so CHAOS 0 keeps SWARM clean however many phones are around. The factory SWARM presets set CHAOS 70–100. A device's own timbre (V10.6v) colours the lead too: the nearest one gives it its FM ratio and depth.

The screen. EDIT 1 / 2 and the SWARM pages show the swarm (each device a square at its note, its place in the image) with a bar under it: the morph, FAR left, NEAR right.

FILTER (every engine)

EDIT > FILTER: TYPE LP, HP, BP, COMB, COMB-, MS20 or LP24 (V10.22: a 24 dB/oct low-pass, two poles in series, RES on the second). COMB and COMB- are a comb tuned to the note you play; CUT moves it, RES is how much it rings), CUT, RES, and KNOB 4: on ANALOG its own DRIVE, on every other engine the track's DIST. On ANALOG the filter is per voice; on MATH the filter page drives its per-voice filter; on the others it is a filter on the track that follows LFO DEST / ENV DEST FILTER.

COMB and COMB- (0.9.9-snd1): the comb is tuned to the newest note (CUT 64 = the note, ±32 semitones either side) and glides when CUT or the note moves (no zipper). RES is how long it rings, the same on every note: from a short colour (25 ms) up to ~9 s near self-oscillation at the top; a soft clip inside the loop keeps the ring bounded and a damping low-pass in the loop (opening with RES) lets the highs die first, like a string. COMB (positive feedback) is metallic and flanger-like; COMB- (negative) keeps only the odd harmonics: hollow, clarinet-like, at the same pitch.

MS20 (0.9.9-snd1): an aggressive 2-pole low-pass after the Korg MS-20: its resonance is fed back through a diode-like clip, so it screams at high RES but stays under control (the clip sets how loud it can ring, and a hot input growls instead of blowing up); RES is exponential (soft at 0, very sharp in the last part of its travel, and the ring still dies out when the part goes silent). Same knobs: CUT (with LFO / ENV DEST FILTER), RES, and the drive of KNOB 4. On ANALOG and MATH the MS20 (like the combs) is the track's filter after the voices: their own filter opens. LP, HP and BP sound exactly as before.

CHORUS (0.9.9-snd2) is lighter: its LFO is read every 64 samples (16, 8, 4 at the fastest RATEs) and the three voices glide between, instead of three sines every sample; it sounds the same (-63 dB from before at the default).

DELAY DRIVE (0.9.9-snd2; DLY 3 KNOB 3, the page's free knob; 0–100): a soft saturation inside the delay's feedback loop. A quiet repeat comes back as it went, a loud one is rounded and pressed down, so every pass gets a little grittier and a little quieter, like a tape or a BBD driven hard; with FEEDBK past 100 it holds the runaway lower. 0 is the delay exactly as before (a project of before opens with DRIVE 0).

5. DRUM-X

DRUM-X is a synthesized kit of eight sounds, BD SD CP CH OH TM RS CB, generated in real time.

  • (0.7.2) Eleven kits as the DRUM engine's presets: DRUM-X (the factory kit), 909 CLUB, 808 BOOM, LOFI DUST, INDUSTRIAL, ACOUSTIC, DUB ROOTS, TECHNO RAW, BREAKS, HIP HOP (V10.34: boom bap, suggests the pattern BOOM BAP), LATIN PERC (V10.7.8: bombo, timbal, llamador, a GUIRO on the closed hat, maracas, conga, clave, cowbell).   • Every sound has two sides, A and B. MORPH (KIT, KNOB 1, also on HOME) moves the whole kit between them: the knob to play live.

  • The pages go the whole kit first, then one sound, then the volumes and the stereo (0.6.58):

The step's own RATCH (SEQ > COMP) repeats its other sounds: a sound with its own x2 / x4 / x8 or recorded ONCE is left out of it.
  • MIDI (0.8.5): notes 35–81 still play the GM drum map exactly as before. The notes around it, which used to play their octave of 36–47 plain, are now these octaves, by pitch class: 23–34 SHORT, 11–22 GHOST, 0–10 FLAM, 82–93 REPEAT 1/2, 94–105 REPEAT, 106–127 x2 (0.9.17; 84 the kick every other step, 96 the kick once a step, 108 the kick x2, 24 the kick SHORT, 114 the closed hat x2).   • With the ARP on, the keys feed the arp plain (no ratchet, SHORT, GHOST or FLAM). The DRUM grid's lane keys are always the centre octave.

6. The sequencer

Each track has its own 64-step pattern with its own length (LEN), division (DIV), swing and gate, so patterns of different lengths turn against each other.

Finding your way: the SEQ chain

(0.9.2) The sequencer's eleven pages (0.9.18: the BAR page is gone) are one chain in five groups, as every section: SELECT walks them a page a detent (no wrap), a SEQ tap jumps to the next group's first page (round), and the tabs at the top are the groups, PATT · TRACK · BAR · STEP · AUTO, the one you are on lit with a dot for each of its pages; the footer says SEQ · BAR · STEP 6/11. Until 0.9.1 SELECT zoomed three levels (PATTERN ↔ BAR ↔ STEP, the LENTE of 0.8.3) and the tap went round each level's sets: the pages and their pictures are the same, only the way between them is new. The pattern's bars (up to four, at LEN 64) are drawn as small cells: the bar you edit tinted violet with its number violet, the number of the bar playing orange, a bar past LEN dark. (V10.6 and after) The chain is STEP · LENGTH · RHYTHM · TRACK · EUCLID · MODES · MUTATE · PLAYHEADS · STRETCH · COMP · COMP 2 · AUTOMATION · AUTO LIST · LYRIC STEP · GENERATE, in eight tabs (STEP · LEN · EUCL · HEAD · STR · COMP · AUTO · GEN): EUCLID and MODES, under EUCL, are where MUTATE and MUTATE 2 were (MUTATE came back inside EUCLID, V10.16). LYRIC STEP shows only on a VOICE track, GENERATE only where the track generator can write the track.

  • PRESETS on a SEQ page loads a pattern: it opens the PHRASES loader with the next pattern picked (KNOB 1 picks too), OCT+ loads it into the track, OCT− goes back to the SEQ page you came from.

  • CHANCE (a step's chance) is reached from code (the dice, the editor); it is drawn as the STEP group.

Writing steps

  • SEQ > STEP on a melodic track is a piano roll. KNOB 1 moves the step cursor, KNOB 2 transposes the step, KNOB 3 is TIME, KNOB 4 is VEL (below). A step holds up to four notes.
  • VEL (KNOB 4, V10.8; it was FLAG: – / ACC / SLD / A+S): 1–127 is the step's velocity (a step with none recorded shows 96, what it plays at). A turn stops at 127; past it, one detent each: ACC (accent, plays at 127), SLD (slide, keeps the velocity), A+S (both). Turning back down from ACC returns to VEL 127. (The DRUM grid is unchanged: STEP, LANE, HIT, ACC.)
  • A note's length (0.8.4): KNOB 3 (TIME) is one range, REST ← TIE ← NOTE → 2 ST → … → 16 ST. On a note, turning right lengthens it: the steps after it become TIEs, one a detent (the card says 3 ST, the roll draws the longer bar); a chord keeps all its notes. Turning left shortens it (its last TIEs become RESTs) down to NOTE, then the step itself becomes a TIE, then a REST, as before (a fast turn stops at NOTE first). It lengthens over RESTs and empty steps only: the next note stops it (the screen says NEXT NOTE; turn that step to TIE or clear it with SEQ + G5 to go on). At most 16 steps or LEN; near the end of the pattern it goes round to step 1, as the sequencer plays it. So any note, recorded or written, can be made longer or shorter afterwards with no REC. (On a DRUM track nothing changes: hits have no length.)
  • Step recording: on STEP, while stopped, press REC (the screen says KEYS WRITE STEPS). Play a note or a chord: it goes into the cursor step, and when you let go the cursor moves on. Not armed, the keys just play.
  • Length: while the keys are held, turn KNOB 1: the note or chord lasts that many steps (1–16, ties after it). It is written when you let go, and the cursor jumps past it. SELECT does the same (0.9.9-seq2c), the note's bar growing on the roll as you turn.
  • Cursor (0.9.9-seq2c): armed and stopped with no key held, SELECT moves the cursor a step left / right (round inside LEN) instead of walking the pages; not armed it walks the pages as everywhere. DRUM-X's grid as before.
  • Arpeggio: while the keys are held, press ARP: the chord is written as an arpeggio into the roll. KNOB 1 is now its resolution (1/16, 1/8, 1/8., 1/4: a note every 1–4 steps), KNOB 2 the order (UP, DOWN, UP-DN, RND), KNOB 3 the octaves (1–3). The screen shows the settings; letting go writes it, the cursor after it. KNOB 4 (and 2–3 without ARP) still makes parameter locks on the step.
  • SEQ held + G5 (CLR STEP, 0.9.2; until 0.9.1 an EDIT tap) clears the cursor step and its locks and moves the cursor on; on NOTE with a note in focus it deletes that note alone. Turning TIME (KNOB 3) down to REST clears the step's notes too.
  • On a DRUM track, STEP is a grid: white keys are the 16 steps of the page, black keys 1–8 pick the lane (and play it), black key 9 held is ACC (white keys then toggle accents), black keys 10 / 11 page down / up when LEN is over 16. Knobs: STEP, LANE, HIT, ACC.

Live recording

Press REC anywhere (not on STEP while stopped): the track is armed and the transport starts. What you play is recorded into the pattern, quantized to its steps, overdubbing. Knob moves on the selected track are recorded as automation. MENU > AUDIO has the CLICK (OFF / REC / ON), CLICK LEVEL and COUNT-IN (OFF, 1 BAR, 2 BARS).

How a note is held is recorded too (V10.6z4): its length goes into the step it ends in (the step's LEN, in 1/16 of a step; a note held to the step's end, or tied, plays the track's GATE), a legato (the next key down before the last one is up) on a LEGATO / UNISON track records a SLIDE so it plays back as one gliding envelope, even when the next note came in late (MONO and POLY: the notes touch, retriggered), and the velocity is kept as played (only 127 marks an accent; 111–126 used to come back as 127). REC Q (100 % by default) snaps where notes start, not how long they are; set it to 0 % (REC + B3) to keep your timing too. A note played in the very first moment after PLAY goes to step 1 (it went to the last step).

Pattern pages

  • COMP (step components v2, 0.9.9-seq2d): up to two per step, COMP the first (C1), COMP 2 the second (C2). Pick the step on the roll (STEP's KNOB 1, or a key on the DRUM grid), then SELECT to COMP. Every component is the same four fields: KNOB 1 ACTION, KNOB 2 LENGTH (1–32: the steps it stays on, from its own on, as the track plays them), KNOB 3 CONDITION (on which passes of the pattern it acts), KNOB 4 VALUE. On the roll a step with components shows a small mark per component on top (violet pitch, orange time, grey flow, white sound), and a thin line of the same colour over the steps its LENGTH covers. (0.9.9-gen3) The cards show every value whole, in short: ACTION as below (STUT, TRANS, RAND), LENGTH as "8 ST", CONDITION as "1:4", "!3:8", "50%", "1ST", the card's label MONO / POLY where the step is either.
  • ARP: an arpeggio over its LENGTH, replacing those steps. VALUE: NOTES (the step's chord) or SCALE (up from its lowest note), the range, ORDER (UP DN UD RND: up, down, up-down, random) and RATE beside it (16 = 1/16, 32T, x1 … x8 past 1/64, in the track's time).
  • SEQ: the note walker. Each pass of the pattern this step plays the pattern's next programmed note (pass 1 its first note, pass 2 the second …): the same notes you wrote, recombined in time. VALUE: the note it starts from, LOOP or PING (bouncing back and forth).
  • OCT+ / OCT-: the steps of its LENGTH 1–3 octaves up / down, then back.
  • RATCH: every step of its LENGTH split into x2 … x8 parts; DECAY FLAT, FADE (each softer) or RISE.
  • STUT (stutter): this step again in place of the next ones (EVERY 1 … 4 steps; the steps between silent).
  • MUTE: its LENGTH of steps silent (CUT: what the track sounds is cut first).
  • TRIG (KNOB 1's last): an Elektron-style trig condition, MUTE the other way round: its LENGTH of steps play only on the passes its CONDITION holds, and are silent on the others (no CUT, no VALUE). The card shows the CONDITION as any: "4:4" plays on pass 4 of every 4. Example: play only on the 4th pass: TRIG 4:4. After it, PRE reads its condition (true on the passes it played); C2 ELSE over a C1 TRIG acts on the passes it plays (it did not mute), a C2 TRIG ELSE plays the passes C1 did not act. Its mark is MUTE's (grey). Also on GLO > COMPS. It is saved as a MUTE with a VALUE of its own: firmware before TRIG loads it as a MUTE CUT with the condition as it is (the opposite passes).
  • TRANS (transpose): the notes moved MOVE degrees (−7 … +7, in the scale). RANGE FIX: whenever its condition holds; RANGE 2 … 16: it adds a MOVE each pass its condition holds, round RANGE places (back to 0), acting every pass.
  • RAND: each note up or down the scale at random, up to 1–7 degrees.
  • LOCK: the step's parameter locks (hold the step and turn, see Parameter locks) only when its condition holds, held over its LENGTH. VALUE shows how many locks the step has.
  • RAMP (V10.6v, in JUMP's place: a JUMP of before is dropped): the step's first parameter lock is the parameter and its target; the value glides there FROM KNOB (the knob's value) or OPP (the other end of its range), over 1–32 steps or 1–16 bars, along a CURVE: LIN, EXP, LOG, S, STEPS, ZIGZAG. It never restarts itself while it runs, and glides back to the knob at its end and on STOP. No lock on the step: nothing. Not on GLO > COMPS.
  • SHIFT: the steps after it played from another place, round within the pattern (a playback map: the steps themselves never move), on the passes its condition picks. CHORD: the step's note played as a chord.
  • SOUND (a DRUM track): SHORT, GHOST or FLAM hits. BEND (kept from before): the note glides ±12 semitones.
  • CONDITION: ALWAYS; 25 / 50 / 75 %; 1ST / !1ST (the first pass after PLAY); FILL / !FILL (FX held); PRE / !PRE (the track's last condition held / did not); NEI / !NEI (the same, the track before); N:M / !N:M (pass N of every M, M up to 16). Passes are counted per track, per lap of its pattern.
  • MONO / POLY: C2's condition ELSE makes the step MONO: one component acts, C1 if its condition holds, else C2 (the card's label says MONO). Any other condition on C2 is POLY: both act, C1 on the step, C2 over the result.
  • Example: four notes on steps 1 5 9 13, each with C1 MUTE at !1:4, !2:4, !3:4, !4:4 (one note a bar), and on step 1 C2 TRANSP +1 RANGE 4 at 1:4, LENGTH 16: a 16-bar melody, every bar different, then again.
  • Projects before 0.9.9-seq2d load migrated: ARP NOTES / SCALE an ARP over the TIEs after it, ARP SEQ the SEQ walker, TRIG a MUTE with the opposite condition, FILL MUTE !FILL, EVERY each component's condition, OCT OCT+ / OCT- (ALT: both slots, 1:2 and 2:2), TONAL a FIX TRANSP to the loop's end, WALK / CLIMB an adding TRANSP, ECHO RATCH (DECAY DOWN; EVEN FLAT), PULSE STUTTER over the empty steps, JUMP / REV JUMP, KILL MUTE CUT, TOUCH SOUND. Not quite the same: KILL's step is now silent; ARP SEQ walks instead of arpeggiating; EVERY beside a TRIG, FILL, alternating OCT or a WALK at a rate is dropped; OCT RND becomes 50 %; WALK / CLIMB UP-DN and RND go UP, CLIMB moves 7 degrees (an octave on a seven-note scale); JUMP x2 jumps once a pass. Saved again, the project is v2 (firmware before seq2d refuses it). The step's own CHANCE and RATCH stay on the CHANCE page.
  • Global components (0.9.9-seq2f): GLO > COMPS (GLO's third tab, after MIX since V10.37) is a lane of 16 steps on the transport's 1/16 (one bar, looping) whose components act on several tracks at once. The white keys pick its step, KNOB 1–4 are ACTION, LENGTH, CONDITION, VALUE as on COMP (actions MUTE, TRIG, STUTTER, OCT+ / OCT−, RATCH, TRANSP, ARP on each track's own notes, RANDOM; conditions as the tracks', without NEI and ELSE; the passes are the bars since PLAY, the lane has its own PRE and its own dice). Black keys 1–6 turn the tracks it applies to on and off (the roll shows them as small marks under each step); COMPS 2: STEP, TRACK, that track ON / OFF, and MODE: POLY (the default) the global transforms the step first and the track's C1 / C2 run on the result (a track's ARP inside a global STUTTER); MONO while a global acts on a track, that track's own components do not. One component a step; its LENGTH counts global steps. An empty lane changes nothing. Example: step 1 STUTTER LENGTH 2 at 4:4 on all tracks, step 3 MUTE LENGTH 14 at 8:8 on T1 alone: every fourth bar stutters, bar 8 drops the drums. The lane is saved with the project (and in each section's slot); projects before seq2f load with it empty.
  • A long chord slide (0.6.83): the glide into a chord lasts as long as that chord, its TIEs included; GLIDE sets how much of it (127: all of it). A chord held over TIEs on slow steps (e.g. DIV 1/4 at 120 BPM: ten steps, 5 s) slides that slowly and smoothly; new notes fade in as they glide.
  • PATTERN (the PATTERN group's first page): LEN (1–64), DIV, SWING, GATE — the pattern's length, its step's note value, its swing and the length of every note (a step's own length: STEP's TIE, or MICRO for its start). GATE is 100 % for new tracks (V10.6z4; INIT, a new project, power-on; was 50 %): notes play their whole step and retrigger the next one (a SLIDE or TIE makes them legato). Projects and user presets keep the GATE they were saved with (one saved at 50 % loads 50 %); clearing steps leaves GATE as it is; GEN sets its own per part.
  • Moving a note or a chord that came in early or late: NOTE, KNOB 1 FOCUS on the step, KNOB 3 MOVE moves it sideways a step at a time (FOCUS ALL: the whole chord with its TIEs; one note focused: that note alone).
  • STRETCH (0.6.85; the PATTERN group, after PATTERN): reshapes the whole sequence of the selected track as the knob turns (0.6.92; it used to wait for OCT+). Every turn works from the sequence as it was before the first one (its base), so x2 then x4 is x4, not x8, and back to the middle is back to where you started; the message says the LEN before and after (LEN 4 > 16); UNDO (GLO + G4) brings it back; editing the result makes it the new base. The other knob takes the result as its base (0.6.96): REPEAT x2 then STRETCH x2 stretches the repeats.
  • KNOB 1 STRETCH: x2 … x8 spreads the steps that many times apart (LEN 4 x4: 16), a held note held all the way, short ones kept short; past 64 steps the end is cut. /2 … /8 squeezes it: of each group of steps the first one that plays stays (LEN 32 /2: 16). x1 in the middle: as it was. The automation and locks move with their steps.
  • KNOB 2 REPEAT: copies steps 1..LEN after themselves, x2 … x8 times or TO 64 (as many whole copies as fit), automation and locks too; OFF at the left: as it was. Four notes become a 64-step bed to record more notes over (live REC overdubs).
  • KNOB 3 NUDGE has three modes (V10.25, V10.7.4), picked with OCT− / OCT+ on this page:
    • OCT− (blue < >): the whole sequence one step right / left, round inside LEN (as TOOLS A3 / B3).
    • OCT+ (red, a number): the cursor step's micro timing, 1/64 of a step a detent (−32 … +31; about 2 ms at 120 BPM), early or late, as REC Q records it. PRESETS moves the cursor to the step; the card shows its value.
    • OCT+ again (red, S and the step's number) (V10.7.4): the cursor step itself, one step right / left a detent (it trades places with its neighbour; a note moves with its TIEs and trades with the neighbouring note whole; at step 1 and at LEN it stays); the cursor goes with it.
  • KNOB 4 ROTATE (−32 … +32; it was SHIFT's OFFSET): the sequence read from another step, without moving the steps themselves — a live, automatable rotation (also on the SCL layer's KNOB 3).
  • MICRO (0.6.92, NOTE's KNOB 4; it was the TIMING page's OFFSET): the focused step's own place off the grid, in 64ths of a step (0.9.1; it was sixteenths), from −32/64 (half a step early) to +31/64 (late): a 64th of a 1/16 step is about 2 ms at 120 BPM. A 64th a detent (turned faster, more: knob acceleration); the card shows +5/64; 0 puts it back on the grid. Early steps really sound early: the step before ends that much sooner and the grid is kept. The piano roll draws the notes where they play. A chord shares its step's offset; moving, stretching or repeating steps keeps it; saved with the project. REC Q (TRACK's KNOB 4) is the recording quantize that writes it: 100 % (the default) live recording snaps to the grid; lower, the step keeps that much of where you played it (0 %: all of it, to the nearest 64th), early or late; 50 % half way to the grid. Projects from before 0.9.1 play exactly as they did (their sixteenths are 4/64 each). A project saved with 64ths still loads on older firmware, which plays each step at the sixteenth at or before its 64ths (up to 3/64 sooner).
  • TRACK (0.6.92; it was SHIFT + HUMAN): the whole track's settings. PITCH (±24 semitones; not on drums). CHANCE: the whole track's chance, 100 % plays every step as written, lower thins it out (each step's own chance, from the dice or the editor, scaled). HUMAN (one amount, 0–100 %): the track plays like a person, each note a little different every time round, the steps staying as written — its velocity up or down at random (up to ±50 at 100 %), its start a little late (up to 40 ms, never more than a third of the step, never early; a chord's notes stay together, a STRUM keeps its spacing), its length longer or shorter (up to ±40 %), and its envelope's DECAY and RELEASE a little longer or shorter (up to ±20). 0 % plays exactly as written (the default). Kept when you change the sound; automatable. A project saved with the old VEL / TIME / GATE loads with the smallest of the three as its HUMAN. REC Q: see MICRO. The old C.LEN (the components as a lane of their own length) is gone: components live on their steps, EVERY says how often they play.
  • AUTOMATION: PLAY turns the track's automation on or off, EVENT and LOCK count the records, CLEAR erases them (OCT+, with a dialog). The automation is what you record by turning a knob while REC is on and the track plays (a value that moves along the pattern), and the parameter locks (a value a step, see below): this page shows how many of each the track holds and whether they play; the page's hint says so. AUTO LIST is where to see and edit each one.
  • AUTO LIST: every automation event and lock as a list. KNOB 1 the row, 2 the step, 3 the parameter, 4 the value.
  • EUCLID and MODES (V10.6, where MUTATE was; MUTATE is gone): a euclidean pattern written live from four knobs, and four amounts that add to it. See EUCLID and MODES below.
  • PLAYHEADS (the PATTERN group; it was FUGUE), after the Fugue Machine: three more playheads (heads 2, 3, 4) read the same steps as the track's sequencer. KNOB 1 HEAD picks one, KNOB 2 RATE (OFF, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, x1, 3/2, x2, x3, x4 of the track's), KNOB 3 DIR (FWD, REV, PING, RND), KNOB 4 INTVL, its interval in degrees of the scale (−14 to +14; a drum track keeps its lanes). A head plays each step's notes with the step's velocity, accent and chance and the track's GATE (no components, locks or ratchets: those are the sequencer's): canons, counterpoint, patterns phasing against themselves. The page draws the steps, the sequencer's playhead and each head where it is. Saved with the section; the heads start and stop with the transport.
  • AUTO LIST: on + ADD LOCK, OCT+ adds a lock; on a record, OCT+ switches it between LOCK and AUTO; SEQ + G5 (DEL REC; until 0.9.1 EDIT) deletes it.

EUCLID and MODES (V10.6)

(V10.6) MUTATE is gone (its pages MUTATE and MUTATE 2, the changes every N bars, ORBIT / DRIFT, KEEP and the anchor, REVERT and BACK TO BEFORE MUTATE?, GLO + A4 FREEZE, GLO + B4 REVERT ALL, SEQ + D5 REVERT, HOME's look up and ring). In its place in the SEQ chain, after TRACK, two pages under a tab of their own, EUCLID (EUCL): EUCLID writes the selected track's pattern as a euclidean rhythm, MODES adds to it. The page's hint: TURN A KNOB: A PATTERN · OCT+ NEW OCT− BEFORE.

EUCLID in one minute

Drums (a DRUM-X track):

  1. Pick the track with ALGORITHM, press SEQ and turn SELECT to EUCLID (or tap SEQ until the EUCL tab lights).
  2. KNOB 4 LANE starts on ALL: turn KNOB 1 DENSITY (1–8) for a whole kit, KNOB 2 STEPS and KNOB 3 ROTATE for its length and groove.
  3. To tweak one sound, KNOB 4 to its lane (BD SD CP CH OH TM RS CB): HITS, STEPS and ROTATE now write that lane only.
  4. SELECT one detent right, MODES: ACCENT, GHOST, RATCHET, VARY.
  5. OCT+ a new variation, OCT− the one before. To undo: GLO + G4, or hold REC alone and let go.

Melodic (any other track):

  1. Set the key first on SCL (ROOT, a SCALE other than CHR): the screen says SCL. Or leave it: EUCLID finds the key of the other tracks (FROM Tn), else picks one (NEW KEY).
  2. SEQ › EUCLID: KNOB 1 HITS (exactly that many notes), KNOB 2 STEPS, KNOB 3 ROTATE, KNOB 4 VARY.
  3. MODES: MOTION (0 a pedal note, higher bigger leaps), HARMONY (chords on the strong beats), OCTAVE, SLIDE.

Parameter locks

On SEQ > STEP (the BAR group), hold a step (a grid key, or the note being entered on the roll) and turn a knob: that step gets its own value of the parameter that knob edits on the sound page you visited last. Tap EDIT while holding the step to clear its locks. Automation events and locks share 64 records per section (all tracks together); when they run out the screen says AUTOMATION FULL. Copy and paste (0.9.9-seq2h)

On SEQ's roll pages (STEP, NOTE, ..; a DRUM track's grid too), stopped or playing, the 16 white keys are the steps of the bar shown (the cursor's bar, as on the grid and COMP):

  • COPY a step: hold its key and turn SELECT (one detent is enough).
  • COPY a range: hold its first and last keys and turn SELECT: every step between them.
  • COPY the bar: hold keys 1 and 16 and turn SELECT.

Scale

ROOT, SCALE and QUANT are global: one scale for the whole song. Whatever track you set them on (knob, SCL layer, MIDI, a project load), every melodic track follows it at once; drum tracks have none. Each track keeps its own TRANS.

  • SCALE: CHR, MAJ, MIN, DOR, MIX, PEN, MPEN, HARM, PHRY, LYD, LOC, MEL, BLUES, WHOLE, DIMHW, DIMWH.
  • QUANT: OFF; SNAP (keys snap to the scale); WHITE (the white keys walk the scale, black keys are silent); SEQ (as SNAP, and the sequencer's notes snap too as they play).
  • SCL > ALL TR, KNOB 4 (0.7.6; SCL 2 until then; it was called STRN) moves every sequence up or down the scale (±14 degrees), on every track at once. It is global and saved with the project: if your notes sound lower or higher than the piano roll shows, look here. When it is not 0 the piano roll says ALL TR and its value, and PLAY says it too. 0 = off.
  • The RESO reverb and the SLICER's pitch steps follow the scale too.
  • (0.8.3) A section keeps its key: ROOT and SCALE are stored with each section, so a song can change key from one section to the next (GEN's PARTS does it for pop genres: the last section a whole step up). When the song brings in a section, the keys, CHORD, ALL TR and the RESO reverb follow its key, also with a DRUM track selected (then the first melodic track's key counts; until 0.8.3 a DRUM track selected kept the key before).

CHORD (0.6.81)

SCL > CHORD makes a key play a chord on the track (after the Omnichord and the Orchid):

  • CHORD: OFF; in the scale (every chord in key, nothing to think): TRIAD, 7TH, ADD9, SUS2, SUS4, built on the key's degree (a key outside the scale takes the scale note below; CHR: the major scale of ROOT); shapes on any key: MAJ, MIN, 7, MAJ7, MIN7, POWER. In a mode of seven notes (MAJ MIN DOR MIX HARM PHRY LYD LOC MEL) the chord is the mode's own on that degree (every other note of the scale). (0.8.3) In the scales of five, six or eight notes (PEN, MPEN, BLUES, WHOLE, DIMHW, DIMWH) every other note made odd stacks (C minor pentatonic's "triad" on C was C F Bb), so each tone is now the interval the chord asks for where the scale has it, else the nearest one it offers: a major or minor third (none: the chord becomes a suspension), a perfect fifth (else diminished over a minor third, augmented over a major one), a minor or major seventh (else a sixth), the ninth. Every tone stays in the scale: a pentatonic key gives triads, sus4 / sus2 chords and sixths (C minor pentatonic: Cm, Eb, Fsus4, Eb/G, Bbsus4; 7TH on F: F7sus4), whole tone augmented chords, the diminished scales diminished and dominant sevenths.
  • VOICING: CLOSE (root position); LEAD (each chord takes the inversion nearest the last one: voice leading, the Orchid's smoothness); OPEN (the second tone an octave up); +BASS (the root an octave down added).
  • STRUM: 0 all at once; up from there, the notes come in one after the other, low to high (up to ~63 ms apart), as a harp.

Letting the key go ends every note of its chord (those still to strum never come). The chord records into the step, live and in step recording alike (0.6.83: step recording took the key's note alone), and the ARP arpeggiates it. The keys of every note sounding light up, the chord's whole shape too: a way to see and learn the chords. With STRUM (0.6.84) a recorded chord goes whole into the one step where the key went down (its later notes no longer spill into the next step), and the sequencer strums every chord step back the same way, low to high (within half the step's gate). STRUM at 0: the steps' chords as before, all at once. A drum track, MONO, LEGATO and UNISON play the root alone. MIDI notes play as they come.

ARP

ARP: MODE, RATE, OCT (1–4), GATE. ARP 2: SWING, PROB, HOLD, ORDER (NOTE / PLAY). Modes: OFF, UP, DN, UPDN, RND, ORD, REPEAT, DNUP, UP+8, CONV, DIVG, PINKY, THUMB, WALK, CHORD, TRNS (the keys and incoming MIDI transpose the track's sequence by their distance from C4, without playing notes). Key latch: with MODE OFF, HOLD ON keeps the notes sounding after you let go; the next chord replaces them.

7. ARRANGE: songs, sections and scenes

The pieces

  • A project is a song. There are 6 projects (V10.6p: 8 before; songs 7 and 8 are gone, their flash is kept for a drum sample bank: move them into 1–6 or keep them in a backup before updating).
  • Each project has 4 sections, A–D. A section is a full snapshot of the music: every track's sound, steps and automation, the DRUM-X kit and its mutes, the punch-in lane, the SLICER, the macros, plus the tempo and global effects.
  • Each project has one arrangement: up to 16 rows, each a section played for 1–64 bars, optionally with a scene, and an END rule.
  • (V10) In a row, each track can play another section's part (FROM) and a variation of it (VAR): see The TIMELINE page.

That's 24 sections in total (6 × 4).

Your first song, step by step

  1. Make a loop: record it (REC), write steps, use EUCLID or GEN (Your first song (5 minutes)).
  2. Store it as section A: hold SAVE and press C4. The screen says STORED A. It works while playing; the store goes to flash when you stop (stop before switching off). To give it a name, save it on SAVE › SECTIONS instead (12).
  3. Make B, C, D: change the music (steps, sounds, a track muted…) and hold SAVE + D4 (STORED B), then E4 (C) and F4 (D). SAVE + F3–B3 plays a section back to check it (playing: on the next bar).
  4. Open the song: stop (PLAY), press SAVE. At power-on it opens on TIMELINE, the first page; if it comes back to another SAVE page, turn SELECT left to TIMELINE. With no rows it says NO ROWS: OCT+ ADDS ONE.
  5. Add the rows: OCT+ adds a row (ROW ADDED): the first is A, 4 bars; each next one is a copy of the row picked, after it (V10.6o). KNOB 1 the row, KNOB 2 its section A–D, KNOB 3 its bars (1–64). For example A 8 · B 8 · A 8 · C 4 · D 8.
  6. Per track (optional): pick the track with ALGORITHM. F4–B4 make it play part A–D in this row (FROM; its row's letter again: back), KNOB 4 its VAR (MUTE, THIN, FILL, HALF, REV, SHIFT, OCT+ / OCT−, VAR 1–4, in this row only); F3–D4 mute T1–T6 in the row (The TIMELINE page).
  7. END and scenes on ARRANGE: turn SELECT right past PARTS, CLIPS, SECTIONS and PROJECTS to ARRANGE (or SAVE + G5). KNOB 1 past the last row is + ADD, then END: there KNOB 2 picks STOP (once), x2–x8 or LOOP. A scene: set the mutes (GLO held + black keys 1–6) and macros you want, KNOB 1 to the row, KNOB 4 right: SCENE STORED (left clears it).
  8. Play it: stopped, press PLAY on ARRANGE. The song plays from the top (a white line shows where); on TIMELINE, PLAY loops the row picked and PLAY again lets the song go on from there (V10.6o); PLAY again stops it and the music you had before comes back. From any other page: hold SAVE + D5 (PLAY: THE SONG) and PLAY plays the song; SAVE + D5 again goes back to PLAY: THE LOOP. A row on an empty section: the song won't start (PATTERN A EMPTY).
  9. Delete a row: stopped, on ARRANGE or TIMELINE, KNOB 1 to the row, OCT−: DELETE SONG ROW?. OCT+ deletes it, OCT− keeps it. It cannot be undone.
  10. Or record it live: SONG REC. Play a section (SAVE + F3), hold SAVE + E5 (SONG REC NEXT BAR; stopped: SONG REC: PLAY), then play the sections in turn with SAVE + F3–B3: each becomes a row with the bars it played and a scene. SAVE + E5 again (or stopping) ends it. It replaces the rows there were.

GEN — the song generator (HOME + SELECT)

Hold HOME and turn SELECT, from any page, or on HOME walk the chain with SELECT (ECO · MACROS · BEING · ROUTES · GEN 1 · GEN 2 · GEN 3) or tap HOME to its next group (ECO → BEING → GEN). (0.9.2) The GENERATOR is HOME's last group, three ordinary pages, no longer a screen of its own: SELECT turns the page, KNOB 1–4 set the four values shown (PRESETS: the GENRE, the generator's preset), OCT+ makes the piece and OCT− goes back to ECO (the piece stays; UNDO, GLO + G4, brings back what was there before); every section button works from there. The panel shows the five parts as rows (the ones the page's knobs weigh lit) with a preview of their density and heat, the FORM's sections as coloured chips, the last piece and OCT+ MAKE. PLAY runs and stops the transport without leaving the screen (0.6.97).

GEN can't generate while a song plays (SONG mode, SAVE + D5, ARRANGE / TIMELINE PLAY, or a quick chain): OCT+ says SONG PLAYS: STOP IT TO EDIT. Stop the song, or turn SONG mode off with SAVE + D5 so PLAY plays the loop.

A part set OFF stays off (V10.8). The piece always has two melodic parts at least, but only parts left on AUTO are filled in for that (BASS first, then CHORDS); before V10.8 BASS and CHORDS came back on even when set OFF. The GENRE is kept between visits (V10.8; it used to go back to EXPLORE every time GEN opened); power-on and NEW PROJECT start on EXPLORE.

The piece is deterministic: the same settings make the same piece every time, so a seed you like can be dialled back. OCT+ with the settings unchanged throws a new seed (another piece); turn a knob and press OCT+ and you get the same piece with that one thing changed — every part draws its random numbers from its own stream, so BASS turned leaves the drums and the lead as they were. The screen shows LAST: HOUSE 0421 (the genre and the seed).

A genre's throws differ (V10.6z10). Each throw of a genre takes its own bass (one of the genre's three archetypes, a few of its steps moved), its own groove (the genre's extra kicks in a place or two of its idiom, the hats and the percussion from the genre's pools where they are not its identity: cumbia's guiro, the dembow's 3 + 3 + 2, jazz's ride stay; now and then a euclid on a percussion lane; IDM's breaks one of four), its own progression (the genre's own a third of the time, else one its harmony's grammar writes), its own sounds (another of the engine's presets of the same type a third of the time) and a tempo around ENERGY's place in the genre's range. In PARTS and SONG the sections develop: their bass, chords and arp a variation of A's, the lead's motif developed another way, a one-chord piece's sections denser or thinner, higher or lower. And OCT+ never throws the core (progression, bass, map, groove) of one of the genre's last eight throws: it tries other seeds first. A seed is still its song.

Leads and drums (0.9.9-gen2). A lead's vibrato is a singer's, not a siren's: LFO PITCH 1 (~19 cents), 5.1–6.3 Hz, faded in over 160–450 ms so the attack is clean, the filter's LFO off under it; otherwise no pitch LFO, only a slow, subtle one on the filter. Pads never get more than a tiny, slow pitch drift (EXPLORE included). The drums leave room for the bass and the lead: every lane's DECAY shorter (the kick's tail, the clap, the hats, the toms most; the 808 and DUB kicks shorter too), the kits' pitch sweep, FM and drive offsets halved, the snare's FEEDBK 2–4, MORPH moving less, GLUE at most 8, the drum track's LEVEL 94 (was 106) and drier sends: over the 43 genres the drums went from 74 % of the mix's energy to 48 %.

EXPLORE (0.8.6), the first choice and where the screen always opens (at power-on, after NEW PROJECT, every time you open GEN), follows no genre and breaks their rules on purpose, so every seed is a very different piece (LAST: EXPLORE 0421): - Tempo anywhere from 56 to 200 BPM; any drum map (the procedural IDM's too) and kit, its sounds pulled far from the kit's; any three bass archetypes against any kick rule; any chord play, voicing, arp and lead; a role may take another role's kind of sound (a plucked bass, a bell lead, an organ texture). - The key: a mode of seven from the SCALE page, the exotic ones likeliest (phrygian, lydian, harmonic and melodic minor, locrian), the lead on a pentatonic, hexatonic or gapped set over it. Harmony: one to four chords on any degrees in any order (non-functional moves), any quality, now and then a borrowed, secondary or flat-side chord (the out-of-key notes), a family and colours thrown too; a PARTS song's last section may move anywhere (a semitone, a tritone ..), not just a whole step up. - Time: a chord 8 to 48 steps, the loop 8 to 64 (odd metres: 3/4, 5/4, 7/8 ..); each track its own LEN and DIV (1/8, 1/32, 8T, 16T: polyrhythm), a track's own swing, drum hits off the grid. - Sound: any engine on any role, ANIMAL and VOICE included (VOICE singing words in ES / EN / JA, a phrase or scat), FM6, ANIMAL and VOICE on two tracks at most; DRUM-X's octaves per sound on the steps (x2 x4 x8, SHORT, GHOST, FLAM); wide pans, glides, vibratos, detune, distortion types (V10.8: glide, vibrato and detune less often and shallower: less pitch wobble). - Motion: step components scattered (RAND, OCT, BEND, ECHO, PULSE, WALK, CLIMB, ARP, TONAL, some every other loop), PLAYHEADS on a track or two; the delay's grain pitch (V10.8: one piece in ten, an octave up or down) and spray, ping-pong, any reverb. - What it keeps: it always plays (two melodic parts at least, the genres' levels, the shared voice budget, the delay short of self-oscillation), the SCALE page and the CHORD page say the key and the chords it plays, and every note is in that key but the coloured chords' own tones and the approaches. The same seed is the same piece; the genres' pieces are untouched by it; a genre's piece after it starts from the clean base (DIV 1/16, no PLAYHEADS).

What a piece is made of:

  • Drums from a map of priorities (after Mutable Instruments' Grids): every step of every lane has a level, a hit where the level clears the density DRUMS sets, so ghosts go first as you turn it down and the kick's anchors last. Then the snare's ghost notes on the best positions (the sixteenth before 2 and 4 first), the hats' accent cell (house, funk, garage, machine), a small variation on the even bars (COMPLEX), the genre's fill on the phrase's last bar (a snare roll, a tom run, the kick dropped, a hat roll, a linear funk fill, Blue Monday's sixteenth kicks, a dub rim, a break before the one) and the snare laid back where the genre plays late (TIMING). ENERGY and the amounts decide how much plays; the kick is never dropped from the one.
  • The key (0.8.3): a mode of seven notes for every genre (its list: major, minor, dorian, mixolydian, phrygian, lydian, harmonic and melodic minor), written to the SCALE page (ROOT, SCALE, and QUANT SNAP unless you chose another QUANT) on every track, so the keys you play afterwards, the CHORD page, ALL TR and the RESO reverb are in the piece's key. A genre that sings pentatonic or blues (deep house, funk, boom bap, D&B, afrobeat ..) gets the mode that holds it (dorian or aeolian; major for the major pentatonic) and its lead keeps to the pentatonic notes: the chords stay real chords and every note is still in the SCALE page's scale. The chord track's CHORD page is set to the piece's chords (TRIAD, 7TH, ADD9, SUS2 or SUS4, as its progression is coloured), VOICING LEAD, a strum where a guitar plays them; CHORD is off on the other tracks.
  • Harmony (0.8.3): each genre has a family (modal loops; seventh-chord loops; functional pop; colour washes; film; jazz) and progressions written as degrees of the mode, each loop ending on the chord that leads back to its first (IV–I, bVII–i, V–i, bII–i ..): i bVII, i iv7, i bII (phrygian), I V vi IV, vi IV I V, I vi IV V, i bVI bIII bVII, the Andalusian i bVII bVI V, the Aeolian bVI bVII i, i7 bIIImaj7 bVII7 iv7, ii7 V7 Imaj7, Imaj7 vi7 ii7 V7, the bossa's Imaj7 II7 ii7 V7, I bVII IV I .. A mode that would turn one of its chords diminished (dorian's vi, lydian's #iv) never takes that progression: the genre's next one, else the mode's own loop. Then the cadences and colours the genre allows: the minor key's dominant raised before its tonic (the leading tone: trance, italo, synth pop, film, jazz), jazz's turnaround (ii–V in the last bar), a secondary dominant on the second half of the chord before its target (V/vi, V/ii, V/IV: indie, disco, jazz, film; with COMPLEX), a major key's IV going minor before I (mixture: synth pop, indie, shoegaze, film), the flat side borrowed in major (bVII, bVI: motorik, indie, soundtrack). Harmonic rhythm by genre: a chord every bar, every two or four bars, or one chord for the whole loop. Chords are triads, sevenths, ninths (never a b9), add9, sus2 / sus4 (a perfect fourth only), sixths, quartal stacks, power chords, recoloured by the genre (plain, rich, suspended, quartal, power, parallel).
  • Bass from about fifty archetypes written against the kick: house offbeat and bounce, deep walking sixteenths, techno rolling sixteenths and octave bounce, Detroit, EBM, italo, psy, UK garage, Jamerson, around the kick, on the one, slap, Good Times, jazz walking and two-feel, boogie, country, ska, tumbao, bossa, rock eighths, punk sixteenths, McCartney, Peter Hook, motorik, angular post punk, indie, one drop, steppers, rockers, dub, boom bap, trap 808s, DnB, jungle, wobble, pedal, fifths, Suicide, Kraftwerk; and acid by the 303's rules (accents on 3–5 steps, slides on 2–3, an octave jump after a slide, the accents varied over the bars). The root at every chord change (a chord that comes in mid-bar gets its root there), the archetype's third, fifth and seventh the chord's own (a borrowed or raised chord's too), approaches by a semitone into the next root on a weak beat (they always resolve: a thinner bass never leaves one hanging), a pickup at the end of the fourth bar; the kick doubled, answered (the bass stays off the kick's steps), interlocked (and a note right after it) or sustained under it, as the genre does.
  • Chords voiced (0.8.3): of each chord's inversions and spreads, the one nearest the last chord, voice by voice (common tones held, the least motion: about 4.6 semitones a change in all, against 5.5 before), in C3..G5, no second below G3 and no close third at the very bottom; close in the hand for stabs and comping, spread (drop 2) for the washes and film, rootless (3 5 7 9) where jazz and deep house have the bass on the root, moved in parallel for techno and impressionism; played held, swelling, stabbed on the offbeats, pulsed, dubbed through the delay, skanked, comped, pushed, as a drone, sparse, broken (UK garage) or trance-gated, each held to its own chord's end.
  • Arp: an order over the voiced chord — up, up and down, down, Alberti, Glass's groups, a Reich pulse, a sequencer, a walk, cycles of 3 or 5 against the bar (polymetric), a trance gate, octaves — in sixteenths or eighths, with holes (ARP), in D3..D5; each chord's figure from its first note; only the chord's tones.
  • Lead (0.8.3: composed, not walked): a one-bar motif — a rhythm cell (a long note and two short, a tresillo, a pickup into three, a dotted push ..) and a contour of steps (a third now and then, a leap turned back by a step, an arch, a fall, a rise or a wave) — developed over the four-bar phrase by a template: a period (antecedent closing open on the 2nd or the 5th, consequent closing on the tonic), a sentence (the motif, its sequence, a continuation climbing to the climax two thirds in, the cadence), A A B A', call and response (the answer the call's contour turned), the techno hook A A A A', or the dub's with a bar of space; one operator on a varied bar (its last note left out, its end moved, a neighbour into its last note). The motif moves to each bar's chord the voice-led way (its first note the chord tone a step from the last note; the same chord: the motif as it was). The rules every lead keeps: a chord tone on beats 1 and 3, on 2 and 4 a chord tone or a passing note that moves by steps, passing and neighbour notes between, mostly steps (60 % of its moves), never more than an octave, a leap past a fourth followed by a step back, a range of about a tenth, the phrase closing on a stable degree (the tonic, the third or the fifth on a chord tone) and held, no minor ninth or tritone over the chord's root, no avoid note held (a fourth over a major third), a borrowed or raised chord's tones followed (the minor key's b7 becomes the leading tone over its V). Or: a hypnotic ostinato on an euclid (its cell's notes on the chord at the beats), a machine's figure moved with the chord, long notes as a guide-tone line (each the chord tone a step or a third on, a short figure stepping into it now and then), a tintinnabuli line (stepwise, a chord tone on each long note, a passing note between). Never above A5 (B5 in a section lifted a step); high notes softer. Each lead, figure and drum pattern is the genre's own for a seed (the same seed in two genres: two tunes).
  • Sounds by recipe a role: sub, acid, Reese, FM bass, rock bass, Hook's chorused bass, wow bass; pad, strings, choir, organ (WHEEL), electric piano, stab, dub chord, pluck, bell, lead, vox lead, chip, keys, drone, noise, texture, hoover, a shoegaze wall, a tremolo guitar. A recipe picks an engine, a factory sound whose name fits, moves its parameters a little, shapes the envelope, the filter (type, cutoff, resonance, the envelope on it), a slow LFO, drive, chorus, glide, the voice mode. The kit is one of seven (909, 808, click, industrial, soft, dub, breaks) written over the factory DRUM-X kit, the drum track's TONE, DECAY, NOISE and DRIVE with it.
  • Mix: level, pan and sends by role and by how wet the genre is; HUMAN sets SEQ > TRACK's HUMAN on every track by the role (hats and leads more, the kick none) scaled by how human the genre is (a band more than a machine); SWING the genre's unless set; DUCK, CLIP and PUNCH on the master; the delay's TYPE, PING-PONG and TIME and the reverb's type the genre's.
  • With TRACKS 6 the sixth track is a texture (a drone, noise, a choir, an organ, strings) in long notes.
  • FORM: LOOP writes one loop (2 or 4 bars). PARTS also stores sections A (the piece), B (a break: no drums, no bass) and C (a drop: the drums denser) and fills ARRANGE with A B A C (PLAY plays the song, as 0.6.81's DICE ALL did). (0.8.3) The sections are one piece in one key: B starts the loop on its predominant (the same chords turned: IV–I–V–vi for I–V–vi–IV; a one-chord loop goes to its subdominant and back), the motif and the figures the same, moved onto those chords; in the pop genres (ITALO, SYNTH POP, INDIE, DISCO) C is a whole step up, a real key change stored in the section with its SCALE page (pop's last chorus). The modal loops (techno, house minimal, dub ..) keep their loop in every section. SONG writes a club form: A the drop, B the intro (drums and bass), C the build (the chords in, their filter opening over the loop, a roll at its end, the delay thrown on the last step) and D the break (no drums, no bass, the chords wetter; 0.8.3: on its predominant, but in the modal loops), in ARRANGE as B C A D C A with 16-bar rows (the drop 32). Ambient and post-rock genres get an arc instead (thin, in, apex: on its predominant). Sections of yours (stored by you, not by GEN) are never overwritten: then the piece stays one loop.

Every piece replaces every track's steps, sounds, kit, mix and automation. Mutes are left as they are. The roles that play go on the first tracks (drums on the DRUM track), so with TRACKS 4 you hear the most important ones.

FORM MY LOOP: your loop made a song

Set FORM to MY LOOP and press OCT+: GEN turns the loop you are playing into a whole song, by the rules of the GENRE you picked. Nothing of your loop changes: its tracks, steps, sounds and mix stay as they are, and they are section A, the song's peak (its drop). Every other section is the genre's, played on your sounds. GEN:

  • listens to your loop: its key and mode (major, minor, dorian, mixolydian, phrygian or harmonic minor; each note weighed by its length, its velocity and whether it falls on the beat; the bass, the lowest notes, tell the tonic). Where your loop does not decide (a power riff with no third, an 808 on its root and fifth), GEN takes the genre's own mode, then the minor one, and the darker one when your loop is distorted (a track's DIST) or the genre is a heavy one: a dark loop stays dark, an E-F riff is E phrygian. DRUM-X counts too where it plays a pitch: a lane with little noise and a long ring (an 808, a tuned tom) and the notes of an EXTENDED kit's chromatic run (KICK / TOM or a lane's CHROM). A loop of drums alone has no key: GEN's own key and progression for the genre are used.
  • harmonises it again with every seed: a chord a bar (a half bar in a one-bar loop) that contains your loop's notes there (its root, third and fifth what sounds most, the bass note one of them, little of yours a semitone off them; a seventh where your loop plays it; power chords in the genres that play them). A quarter of the seeds keep the chords heard in the loop; the others draw each bar's chord by how well it fits, the genre's own chords and, in a dark loop, the minor ones more likely: another progression under the same loop, still in its key and mode.
  • adds the parts your loop lacks, only on empty tracks (no note; never on yours), by BASS, CHORDS and ARP on GENE 2 (AUTO: the genre's; OFF: none): a bass where your loop has none, chords on that seed's progression where it has none, and on the arp's track a counter-melody made of your melody: the same notes moved an eighth or a beat, its first half answered, turned upside down round its first note or kept to the beats, an octave away, in your key (no melody in the loop: an arp of the chords). Their sounds and mix are the genre's. No empty track: nothing is added and the screen says NO EMPTY TRACK: ARRANGED (the other sections are still the genre's, on your sounds).
  • brings the genre into the song around the drop: B, the verse, plays the genre's drums (its own pattern: REGGAETON's dembow, HARD BASS's kicks, ...) on your kit, and the genre's bass, chords, arp and lead on your tracks' sounds, over the genre's own progression in your key (its borrowed chords and colours included); C, the build, the genre's parts on the drop's chords, its drums the verse's or your loop's (half the seeds) over four bars with a roll on the snare, clap or rim getting faster and louder, the kick out at the end, the master's high-pass opening, the chords' filter rising, a stutter into the drop; D, the break, no drums and no bass, on the genre's chords or the drop's from its predominant, wetter, the synths an octave up on its last bar.
  • arranges it with SONG's rows, each seed its own: the club's plan or an arc (an arc more often in the genres that play one), the verse in the groove and in some of the intros and outros, the rows' lengths, each section's energy (how busy the arp, the lead, the chords' pulse); the intro and the outro with the drums thinned (the off-beat sixteenths out) and few layers, the layers coming in by the genre's priorities, fills at the phrases' ends (ARRANGE's FILL), and every drop row your whole loop as it is. RANDOM picks the genre by your tempo and drums (four kicks a bar from 100 BPM HOUSE, over 138 BPM HARD TECHNO, else KRAUT from 110 BPM, DREAM POP below); EXPLORE picks a different genre each seed, one whose tempo is near your loop's, and harmonises more freely. Muted tracks of yours stay muted. The tempo and the swing stay your loop's.

It is a SONG as GEN's are: PLAY plays it (TIMELINE and ARRANGE show its rows), SAVE keeps it (all its parts and its rows, as any GEN song: a draft until you save it). OCT+ again throws another seed: another song from the same loop (the parts it added are made again). UNDO (GLO + G4) brings your loop back as it was, in LOOP mode, the song gone (from a song you had saved, open it again from SAVE > PROJECT: it is still there). An empty loop: THE LOOP IS EMPTY, nothing made.

Drums on a DRUM track, else the role its steps sound like (low notes: a bass line, chords: a pad, else a lead or an arp). Never the same part twice in a row. Its sound, mix and automation stay.   • LFO + SELECT: the track's LFO: a rate in the middle of the range, any wave, MODE FREE or TRIG (half the time each; TRIG at any phase), a fade now and then, aimed mostly at the filter, a little at the shape, seldom at the pitch or the level.
  • FX + SELECT: the track's drive (half the time none), its chorus, delay and reverb sends, an AUTO PAN one time in three, and the shared reverb's kind, size and damping and the delay's time and feedback (pitched one time in ten, an octave up or down; V10.8, was one in five, with fifths and fourths).

Macro dice

With the macros open (LFO held) or latched: F3 rolls new macro routes on the tracks that are sounding; G3 clears them. See 10.

UNDO (GLO held + G4)

One step back over the last big change. Pressing it again goes forward (redo). The transport keeps playing. (V10.7) REC held alone does the same: hold REC past HOLD (~0.5 s) and let go without touching anything else (no key, knob, button or OCT): UNDO; hold it alone again: redo. Used with anything else it stays the REC layer (CLICK, REC Q, A3 …), and a REC tap still arms recording. GLO + G4 still works. G4 breathes on the GLO map when there is something to undo; with nothing, it says NOTHING TO UNDO.

UNDO covers: DICE ALL, a sound diced, a track cleared (REC F3, TOOLS CLEAR SEQ), CLEAR ALL SEQUENCES (SEQ + REC), NEW PROJECT, INIT and RECALL (EDIT layer or TOOLS), clearing the automation (SEQ > AUTOMATION), and a section loaded with SAVE > SECTIONS > LOAD. It restores the music as it was: sounds, steps, automation, the kit, the punch-in lane, the macros, the tempo and global effects.

UNDO does not cover: section jumps, quick chains and RECALL from the SAVE layer, switching project with the SAVE layer's KNOB 1, CLEAR SONG, deleting a row, SONG REC, anything saved (sections, user presets), user preset loads, sounds picked with PRESETS, SEQ TOOLS actions, or ordinary knob turns. It is not available while a song plays (SONG PLAYS: STOP IT TO EDIT). It keeps only one step, in RAM: it's gone after power-off.

9. Effects and master

FX's chain: TRACK · BUSES · DELAY · REVERB · CHORUS · SLICER · PUNCH · MASTER

(0.9.2; 0.9.9-snd2: MASTER last, the end of the chain) FX is one flat chain of 16 pages in eight groups, its tabs TRK · BUS · DLY · REV · CHO · SLC · PCH · MST: SELECT walks every page (SENDS → DIST → AUTO PAN → BUSES → DLY → DLY 2 → DLY 3 → REVERB → REVERB 2 → CHORUS → SLICER → SL BUS → SL BUS 2 → SL PITCH → PUNCH → MASTER), an FX tap jumps to the next module (round), and there is no module picker any more (until 0.9.1 SELECT zoomed TRACK ↔ BUSES ↔ EFFECT, PRESETS picked the module and the FX tap its page). PRESETS loads the track's sounds, as everywhere. The pages, their knobs and their pictures are the same.

Per track

  • FX: DIST amount and the three sends (CHORUS, DLY, REV).
  • DIST: amount, TYPE (SOFT, HARD, FOLD, CRUSH, RECT, AMP), TONE (darker / brighter). AMP is a guitar amp: two valve stages that clip unevenly (low DIST clean, then crunch, then lead), the bias breathing with the level, and a speaker cabinet with its presence peak; TONE moves the cabinet's brightness.
  • AUTO PAN: the track's pan swung left and right around its PAN. DEPTH (0 = off), RATE, SYNC (BEAT: RATE in divisions 4BAR … 1/32, locked to the bar; FREE: RATE in Hz), WAVE (SIN, TRI, SAW, SQR, S&H).
  • FILTER: see 4.

SLICER (one, on a bus)

  • SLICER: MODE OFF, GATE, STUT; PAT 1–16; RATE 1/8 … 32T; DEPTH.
  • SL BUS / SL BUS 2: ON / OFF for what goes in: T1–T6 and FX (the delay, reverb and chorus returns). Everything on chops the whole mix after the effects; with FX off the tails ring free.
  • SL PITCH: the repeats follow a 16-step pitch sequence (±24 semitones). KNOB 1 the step, KNOB 2 its pitch, LEN, QUANT (CHR or SCL: the song's scale).
  • Each section keeps its own SLICER settings. With LOAD ECO there is no SLICER (its tracks go straight to the mix).

Delay

  • DLY: TIME (synced divisions 4BAR … 1/32, or free times from 5 ms to 2.95 s; V10.8: the dotted 1/4D, 1/8D, 1/16D too, placed by length: 4BAR, 2BAR, 1/1, 1/2, 1/4D, 1/4, 1/8D, 1/8, 1/16D, 8T, 1/16, 16T, 1/32; saved projects keep their times), FEEDBK, TONE (below the middle darker, above thinner), MIX. FEEDBK behaves like an analog delay's (a tape, a PT2399; 0.6.86, 0.6.89): with TONE in the middle (50 %) each repeat is a copy of what went in; at 100 % the echoes hold their level for a long time (they fade very slowly and are never cut off), and past 100 % they grow into themselves and hold, saturating softly instead of clipping (turn it back down to let them die). Up to 80 % it is as before. TONE is resonant: from the middle down a low-pass sweeping from ~8 kHz to ~250 Hz, from the middle up a low cut sweeping from ~70 Hz to ~3 kHz, both with a peak at the cutoff, so the repeats get darker or thinner and narrow to a band each pass (dub style), and at either end with FEEDBK high the loop self-oscillates at that band.
  • Long times (0.8.4): every division plays its real length up to 2.97 s, the longest (before, anything past 1.49 s was cut to 1.49 s: 1/2 below 81 BPM, 1/1 below 162 BPM, 2BAR, 4BAR). To fit them the delay's line runs at three rates, chosen by TIME on its own:
TIME Line Top end of the repeats
up to 0.74 s full rate full range, as before
0.74 s – 1.49 s half rate flat to ~9.5 kHz, -3 dB at ~10.4 kHz
1.49 s – 2.97 s a quarter rate flat to ~4.7 kHz, -3 dB at ~5.2 kHz

So the longer the time, the darker the tail, like a tape or bucket-brigade delay. Only the top end is lost: there is no grit or aliasing (anything folded back is more than 70 dB down) and no extra noise. The rate is applied once, so the repeats do not get darker with each pass (only TONE and FEEDBK do that). MOD, the GRAIN delay, PING, WIDE, TYPE and TONE work the same at every rate. On the two slower rates the first echo comes ~1 ms (half) or ~4 ms (quarter) late; the repeats stay exactly TIME apart. When TIME crosses 0.74 s or 1.49 s (knob, tempo, automation, a project or section load, an external clock) the delay fades out over 3 ms and starts again empty. That leaves a short gap: the echoes already in the line are dropped, but there is no click. A clock that wavers around a boundary does not restart it again and again. Long echoes come back at full level even when nothing else is playing. - DLY 2: WIDE (the right echo later), and the GRAIN delay: PITCH (±12 semitones per repeat) and SPRAY. With LOAD LITE or ECO the GRAIN delay plays as a plain delay (the card says LITE / ECO). MOD (0.6.86): the tape's drift, slow WOW and fast FLUTTER on the repeats (0, the default: none, exact copies; ~20 a tape's; up to a seasick chorus); the repeats stay bright while they drift (0.6.89); saved with the project. - DLY 3 (0.6.90): TYPE: ANALOG (the default: the soft saturation, the resonant TONE, FEEDBK past 100 % self-oscillating) or DIGITAL (clean repeats, TONE a plain filter, FEEDBK 100 % at most: past it the repeats just hold, never grow). PING: ON sends the repeats left, right, left … across the stereo field, each one FEEDBK softer (TIME up to 1.49 s since 0.8.4, the right side at twice TIME; WIDE and the GRAIN delay out of it). Saved with the project.

Reverb (five models)

REVERB > TYPE picks the model; SIZE is the decay on every model. KNOB 3 and 4 change meaning with the type:

TYPE What it is KNOB 3 KNOB 4
ROOM Classic room DAMP PRE (pre-delay, ms)
SPRING Amp spring tank DAMP PRE
SHIMR (shimmer) Rises an octave on every pass DAMP AIR (±24 st: darker and lower / airier and higher)
RESO Four strings tuned to the song's scale FOLLOW (OFF / T1–T6: the chord follows the lowest note that track plays) PITCH (the chord 0–14 steps up the scale)

Since 0.8.4 coming back to CLOUD starts an empty cloud, a frozen one too (it used to resume the cloud left from before).

Chorus

CHORUS: RATE, DEPTH, STEREO (0 as always, down to mono, up to about twice as wide). Hidden with LOAD ECO (a track's chorus send card then says ECO).

MASTER

  • CLIP: saturation that adds character, not volume.
  • PUNCH: fattens the DRUM tracks.
  • DUCK: the kick pulls the rest down; REL is its release.

DJ FILTER (0.9.1, GLO › LIVE › MANO KNOB 4; on MIXER until V10.36)

One knob on the whole mix, just before CLIP and the leveler. In the middle (OFF) it is out of the chain: nothing is processed and the sound is exactly as without it. Turned left it is a low-pass sweeping down (darker: from ~15 kHz a detent off the middle to ~70 Hz at the end); turned right a high-pass sweeping up (thinner: from ~30 Hz to ~6 kHz). The sweep follows the ear (each detent the same musical step), with a mild resonance that grows a little toward the ends. The card shows the cutoff and the side (1.2k LP, 400 HP, OFF) and a small response curve where the bar would be. - It glides: the cutoff follows the knob in ~6 ms, no zipper; going into the chain, out of it and from one side to the other it crossfades (~6 ms), so crossing the middle never clicks. Turning the knob, it stops once in the middle. - Automation: armed and playing, turning it records it on the selected track (SEQ › AUTOMATION, AUTO LIST shows DJ FILT rows; automation only, no lock); a stop or the loop's restart brings back where the knob was. The editor can set and record it too. - Saved with the project (in the place a never-used global had: every older project loads with it OFF). Firmware before 0.9.1 loads a newer project without its DJ FILTER and its automation. - Cost: nothing in the middle; on, about 200 instructions a sample on the FM-1 (the CPU TEST song +1.7 % on the host).

10. Modulation and macros

  • ENV DEST: the envelope to FILTER, PITCH, SHAPE; LOOP ON: with the note held, the envelope goes back to the attack when it reaches sustain (an ADSR-shaped LFO).
  • ENV 2 (V10.22, after ENV DEST): a second ADSR per voice that goes nowhere by itself: pick SRC ENV2 on a MOD slot and send it to any DEST, like the LFO.
  • LFO: RATE, WAVE (SIN, TRI, SAW, SQR, S&H, DRIFT: V10.36, a smooth random wander for drones and pads that breathe; with RATE 4BAR … 16BAR it drifts slowly), MODE, FADE; LFO 2: PHASE. LFO DEST: PITCH, FILTER, SHAPE, AMP.
  • RATE is free in Hz; turn it left past the slowest and it goes in time with the tempo (16BAR and 8BAR, V10.27, then 4BAR, 2BAR, 1BAR, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8D, 1/4T, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32), locked to the bar while the transport plays (in TRIG, PHASE is its offset), in either MODE: a note never moves it then.
  • MODE TRIG: a new phrase (a note with none held) restarts the LFO at PHASE (LFO 2) and fades it in again over FADE; a note played legato over another does not. MODE FREE: no note restarts anything: the LFO just runs, and FADE fades it in only once, when the project loads or when you turn MODE to FREE. In FREE, PHASE is dimmed: the phase TRIG had is kept for when you go back to TRIG. (Until 0.9.18 FREE was PHASE turned left past 0, and FREE still faded in again on every phrase; projects keep their mode: PHASE FREE opens as MODE FREE.)
  • MOD matrix: 4 slots per track (KNOB 1 picks the slot).
  • Sources: OFF, LFO, ENV, VEL, KEY, RAND, MODW (MIDI CC1), AT (aftertouch), EXPR (CC11), M1–M4, STEP (MSEQ), NOISE (a smooth random wander, a new value about every 46 ms: a living wobble on the filter, the pan, a send).
  • Destinations: PITCH, CUT, SHP, AMP, PAN, DIST, CHO, DLY, REV, RATE (LFO rate), VIB, E1–E8 (the engine's own EDIT knobs, shown by name), CLIP and PNCH (the master), and (0.7.0) the FX buses: D.TIM D.FB D.TON D.MIX (DELAY TIME, FDBK, TONE, MIX), R.SIZ R.DMP (REVERB SIZE, DAMP), C.RAT C.DEP (CHORUS RATE, DEPTH). AMT 64 at a full source = the parameter's whole range; every track's slots on the same bus add. A STEP source (the mod sequence) on D.TIM sequences the delay time.
  • MSEQ: a 16-step modulation sequencer per track, like a CV sequencer. KNOB 1 picks the step, KNOB 2 sets its level, LEN and SLEW; DIV on MSEQ 2. Use it from MOD with SRC STEP.

Macros M1–M4

  • Hold LFO: KNOB 1–4 become M1–M4, and the map shows where each one goes ("NO ROUTE: MOD SRC" if nowhere).
  • Let go: they stay latched — KNOB 1–4 keep moving the macros on any page — until you press LFO again.
  • Every power-on starts with random routes (two per track) on tracks that have none. F3 rolls new routes on the tracks that are sounding; G3 clears them. (V10.8) Random routes (power-on, F3, GEN, the dice) never aim at PITCH: a macro drifting the pitch put whole tracks out of tune. Set a PITCH route by hand on LFO > MOD.
  • Routes are ordinary MOD slots (SRC M1–M4): edit them by hand on LFO > MOD. Macro values are saved with each section.

11. Performance

Live sequences: GLO › LIVE (0.9.11)

The six sequences side by side, and modifiers you play over them like MIDI effects on a master: divide, multiply, shorten, reverse, thin, repeat. They never touch the steps: they change how the sequencer walks them, and a track always comes back in phase. Two pages, the first group of GLO (LIVE · MIX · COMPS · GLOBAL · SYSTEM, V10.37: the performing pages first): a GLO tap inside goes round the groups, SELECT left of MIXER reaches them; at power-on GLO still opens the MIXER.

RIELES (rails): each track's loop across the screen at its own length, its playhead (orange) running at its own speed, the ghost (violet dots: where the track would be if nothing were done to it) and the window of CORTE or LOOP framed in violet (the steps outside it dim). The numbers at the left: ALGORITHM's track on the orange badge, the tracks the knobs act on in violet with a bar.

  • KNOB 1 PISTAS: which tracks the other three act on: ALL, then T1 … T6 one by one, then SYN (the synths) and DRM (the drum tracks). The track you edit is still ALGORITHM's.
  • KNOB 2 RATE: /4 /3 /2 2/3 · x1 · 3/2 x2 x3 x4 (2/3 dotted, 3/2 triplets). It goes in on the next beat.
  • KNOB 3 CORTE: the first N steps only (32 … 1, OFF). On the next 1/16.
  • KNOB 4 DENS: to the left THIN (90 … 10 % of the steps play; the same steps on the same loop every PLAY), in the middle everything, to the right RATCH (every step x2 … x4, never a part shorter than ~1.4 ms). On the next 1/16.

MANO (hand): the keys become a board; the rails small on top, the board under them (held: orange; latched: violet).

  • Black keys 1–6 (F#3 … F#4): hold them to pick the tracks (several at once); none held, or A#4: all of them.
  • White keys, on while held, back in phase when let go: F3 /2, G3 x2, A3 TR (3/2), B3 DT (2/3), C4 … F4 LOOP 8 4 2 1 (the N steps the playhead is in; the shortest held wins), G4 REV, A4 PING, B4 C5 RATCH x2 x3, D5 THIN 50 %, E5 F5 OCT+ OCT− (the sequence's notes an octave up / down), G5 RETURN. They go in on the next 1/16 (the rate keys too).
  • G#4 LATCH: then a white key turns its modifier on and the next press off (for the tracks held at the time). C#5 / D#5 GIRO: the playhead a step back / on (a rotation). F#5 RND: the steps in any order (on / off).
  • KNOB 1 RATE, 2 LOOP, 3 THIN on the tracks held (none: all), latched as any knob; KNOB 4 the DJ FILTER, the same as MIXER's KNOB 4.
  • On MANO the keys play no notes; on any other page they play again (a key still held keeps its modifier until it is let go).

On both pages: OCT− tapped is RETURN: the tracks picked lose every modifier and land on their ghost on the next beat. OCT− held is BYPASS: the original while you hold it, every track; let go, the modifiers are back. OCT+ is COMMIT: it asks (WRITE LIVE CHANGES?) and OCT+ again writes what plays into the tracks: RATE into DIV when one fits (x2 of 1/16 = 1/32, 3/2 = 16T, /2 = 1/8), else STRETCH (/2 /3 /4 slower, x3 x4 faster); CORTE into LEN (a LOOP's steps moved to the start); GIRO into ROTATE; THIN into the track's CHANCE. REV, PING, RND, RATCH, OCT and a rate with no DIV or STRETCH (2/3 of a 1/16) stay live. UNDO: GLO + G4.

How the two pages combine: they drive the same modifiers, one set per track. The knobs of both pages are one latched layer: RATE is the same value on both; MANO's LOOP and RIELES' CORTE are the same window (LOOP counts from where the playhead is, CORTE from step 1; the last one turned wins); THIN and DENS are the same density. MANO's keys sit on top while held or latched: a rate key replaces the rate, a LOOP key shortens the window (the shortest wins), THIN takes the thinner, RATCH the larger; REV, PING and OCT are the keys' alone. MANO is the hands-on face (momentary, several tracks at once), RIELES the latched, visual one.

Recording: with REC armed, a note played while its track is modified lands where the ghost is (where the step is in the written pattern), so recording over a modified track never scrambles it; the automation and the locks follow the step that sounds.

Never saved: the modifiers are performance state, like the punch-in FX; STOP keeps them, NEW PROJECT clears them. What you COMMIT is what stays. They cost nothing in the audio render, a few instructions a track in the sequencer.

The FX layer's 1/2 TEMPO and LOOP 3 / 5 / 7 are these same modifiers: 1/2 TEMPO is RATE /2 (or slower, if LIVE already has the track slower), a LOOP the last N steps (the one playing the last of them), in at once on the 1/16 the punch-in starts on, and let go the track lands on its ghost, where it would have been. With a slower RATE (or 1/2 TEMPO) the PLAYHEADS (SEQ › PLAYHEADS) slow down with the track and, back in phase, skip what they missed; a faster one leaves them in time.

Punch-in FX (hold FX)

Effects that act while their key is held and go back exactly when you let go. FX > PUNCH (0.6.99) is their sequencer: the lane that records them. LEN its length, 1 … 64 steps; RES the step, 1/8, 1/16 or 1/32 of a beat (64 steps of 1/16 are four bars, of 1/32 two, of 1/8 eight); KNOB 3 says what it is doing (REC while recording, PLAY while it plays, else how many steps hold an effect); CLEAR (KNOB 4, then OCT+) empties it. The page draws the lane's cells in the effects' colours with the playhead on them, the last effect's name and the tracks it acts on (ALL, SYN, DRM). To record: REC armed and playing (not in a song), hold FX and the effect's key; the steps it passes get it; G5 held erases where the playhead passes. Saved with the section.

  • On the audio: REPEAT 1/8, 1/16, 1/32 (F3 G3 A3), LPF (B3), HPF (C4). REPEAT starts on the next 1/16 while playing. Below about 81 BPM, REPEAT 1/8 is too long for its buffer and is shown dimmed.
  • On the notes: OCT− / OCT+ (D4 E4), 1/2 TEMPO (F4), DEC− (short, plucky) / DEC+ (long) (G4 A4), STUTTER 1/8 / 1/16 (B4 C5), ATK+ (slow attacks, drums too) (D5), ARP (E5; on a DRUM track a new fill every press), RANDOM (F5). They come in on the next 1/16. A#4 chooses which tracks they touch: ALL, SYN (synths) or DRM (drums); the header shows it.
  • Knobs while FX is held: FILTER (left low-pass, right high-pass), CRUSH, THROW (sends the dry mix into the delay and reverb), DEPTH (the REPEAT's level). (0.9.9-snd2) While THROW sends, and for 12 s after (its tail), the delay's repeats go through a band, tape-echo style: a low cut at ~180 Hz and a low-pass at ~4.8 kHz that closes to ~3 kHz as FEEDBK goes up and further (~1.8 kHz) over the tail: a thrown drum loop rings out as echoes instead of a wash of hats and snare noise (each pass loses the noise much faster than the notes). The delay without THROW is as before.
  • Black keys 1–6: mute T1–T6 while held (not saved).
  • (0.9.9-snd2) G5 CLR AUTO (named on the map): FX + G5 empties the punch-in lane (its automation) and nothing else, stopped or playing; while recording it still erases only where the playhead passes. FX > PUNCH's KNOB 4 is CLR AUTO too (the same, with a dialog), and the PUNCH page's lane line is in the large letters.
  • (0.9.9-snd2) G#4 LOOP 3, C#5 LOOP 5, D#5 LOOP 7: while held, every track (of those A#4 picks) repeats its last 3, 5 or 7 steps (from the step playing when it comes in, on the next 1/16), not on the bar: odd loops that turn against the 4/4. Let go and each track is back where it would have been. F#5 GLITCH (from 0.9.18): the master cut on the 1/16s, each one live, repeated, reversed, an octave up or down, gated, skipping or crushed, by its place in a 2-bar cycle (the same every time, so it grooves); here at its default settings.

The punch-in lane: each section has a lane of 64 steps of 1/16 (4 bars). With REC armed and playing (not during a song), the note effects (D4–F5) you hold are recorded into it, one per step (REPEAT, LPF, HPF and the mutes are not recorded). Hold FX + G5 while playing to erase where the play head passes; stopped, FX + G5 erases the whole lane. REC held + FX also clears it (along with the track's FX automation).

Lock: hold FX until its map opens and let go without touching anything: the FX layer stays open ([FX] LOCKED), as the macros do with LFO; the keys play the effects (momentary: held on, let go off), the knobs are FX's macros and OCT− is ALL OFF, with both hands free. Press FX again to unlock (latched effects you left on stay on). Held with keys or knobs, FX works as before and closes when let go.

FX LATCH (MENU > CONTROL > FX LATCH, OFF by default; ON by default from 0.6.44 to 0.6.63): ON, a key pressed with FX toggles its effect on and off, the knob macros keep their values, FX + OCT− turns everything off (FX ALL OFF). Latched effects are not recorded into the lane.

Mutes and solos

  • GLO held: black keys 1–6 mute T1–T6 (latched, saved with the project and in scenes); F3–D4 solo while held; black keys 7–10 mute the DRUM-X groups; E4 unmutes everything.
  • (0.9.1) MIXER's KNOB 4 is the DJ FILTER now: a track is muted from the GLO layer (above), as before; the MIXER still shows the MUTE badge on a muted track.
  • EDIT held on a DRUM track: black keys 1–8 mute each DRUM-X sound.
  • FX held: black keys 1–6 mute while held.

Live layers and the song

The SAVE layer (section jumps on the bar, quick chains, store, recall, SONG REC) is described in 7. GLO + PLAY restarts from the top without stopping.

12. Saving and loading

This is the chapter to read carefully. JIANT keeps several kinds of data in different places, saved in different ways.

The big picture

  • The music is what's playing now, in RAM: all six tracks' sounds and steps, automation, effects, tempo.
  • Sections (A–D of each of the 6 projects) are where you save music permanently, in flash.
  • The arrangement (rows, END, scenes) belongs to the project and saves itself.
  • User presets (32 slots) save single sounds.
  • Settings (the MENU, TRACKS, LOAD) save themselves.
  • The autosave is a safety copy of the music, so you find it again after power-off. It is not a save into a section, and it does not happen at power-off.

How the autosave really works

  • It writes a copy of the music after 10 seconds without changes, only when the transport is stopped, no key or button is held and nothing is sounding, and at most once a minute.
  • It does not save when you switch off. If you change something and power off within those seconds, that last change is lost. To be safe: stop, wait ~10 s, or better, save into a section.
  • At power-on the device starts at zero, as NEW PROJECT leaves it (V10.6e: T1 DRUM-X, T2 ANALOG, T3 PHASE, T4 FM6, T5 VOICE, T6 ANIMAL, each its preset's sound, dry; no steps). With MENU > SYSTEM > RESTORE LAST ON the autosave becomes the music instead (the screen says RESTORED). RESTORE LAST is OFF by default since 0.6.40: no autosave at all.
  • After a crash, a hang or a failed start, the autosave is not restored (the music that was playing could be what crashed).

To save a project section

  1. Stop the transport (saving needs it stopped: otherwise STOP TO SAVE).
  2. Press SAVE and turn SELECT to SECTIONS (the SONG group, fourth page), or tap SAVE again for PROJECTS (the PROJECT group).
  3. On PROJECTS, KNOB 1: the project (1–6), its tile framed; KNOB 2 its section. Turning KNOB 1 does not change the music you're playing — it only chooses where to save. (It switches projects, so do this stopped.)
  4. On SECTIONS, KNOB 1: the section A–D, drawn large with its name (or USED / EMPTY) and NOW on the one the music came from (its name on the big card).
  5. KNOB 4 to the right: SAVE is picked ([OCT+]). Press OCT+.
  6. If the section already holds something: OVERWRITE PROJECT A? (with its name). OCT+ YES.
  7. The NAME screen opens with a name ready. Edit it or not, then OCT+ writes. OCT− cancels and writes nothing.
  8. The screen says SAVED.

A section stores the music including the tempo and global effects. The project's name is its section A's name.

To erase a section (V10.17)

  1. Stop the music. SAVE › SECTIONS, KNOB 1 to the section.
  2. KNOB 2 to the right: ERASE is picked. Press OCT+: ERASE SECT B? (with its name).
  3. OCT+ erases it (OCT− cancels): the section is EMPTY again, in flash too (its chip goes dark on PROJECTS). The music you are hearing stays as it is; ARRANGE / TIMELINE rows that play that section keep playing the music as it is until you store something there again.

To load a section

  1. SAVE's PROJECTS: KNOB 1 the project, KNOB 2 the section (or SECTIONS: KNOB 1 the section).
  2. KNOB 3 to the right (LOAD), press OCT+. The screen says LOADED.

LOAD stops playback and replaces the music: sounds, steps, automation, the kit, the punch-in lane, the macros, and the tempo, effects, MIDI routing and clock settings stored with it. The arrangement stays. If you didn't mean it, GLO + G4 (UNDO) brings back what you had.

Quick store while playing (SAVE layer)

Hold SAVE and press C4–F4 to store the music into section A–D of the current project, or OCT+ to store into the section playing. This works while playing, without naming. The store is in RAM at once and goes to flash when you stop. If you power off before stopping, it's lost — so stop before switching off.

Copying

  • A section to another letter: LOAD it, then SAVE it into the other letter.
  • A section to another project: LOAD it, turn KNOB 1 to the other project (the music stays), SAVE into a letter.
  • "Save as" a new project: with your music playing, stop, go to SAVE's PROJECTS, turn KNOB 1 to an empty project and SAVE into A (SAVE > PROJECTS).

Switching projects

  • On SAVE > PROJECTS, KNOB 1 changes project but keeps the music (to store it there or load one of its sections).

Saving FAQ

I switched off and my last changes are gone. The autosave only writes after 10 s of no changes while stopped, at most once a minute, never at power-off. Stop and wait a moment, or save into a section.

It says STOP TO SAVE. Saving into a section or a user preset writes flash, which pauses the audio for a moment, so it only happens stopped. Press PLAY to stop and try again (the NAME screen stays open; PLAY there only stops).

It says SAVED (NO FLASH). The flash chip wasn't detected, so the save only went to RAM. It survives a reset, not a power-off. Restart the device; if it keeps saying it, see 16.

It says SECTION 90% FULL / TOO FULL TO SAVE / SECTION FULL. A section has a fixed size. Very busy music (six tracks of chords on all 64 steps plus lots of automation) can outgrow it. Saving a section that doesn't fit says SECTION FULL and leaves the stored one untouched. Shorten patterns, clear unused steps or automation.

I stored with the SAVE layer while playing and lost it. Layer stores reach flash when you stop. Stop before switching off.

Is my arrangement saved? Yes, automatically, a couple of seconds after each change while stopped.

Where's my drum kit in a user preset? User presets don't store the DRUM-X kit; save the project section instead.

Can I back everything up? Yes: the web editor's complete backup includes all 6 projects (a backup made before V10.6p holds 8: restoring it brings 1–6 back and says which stayed in the file) with their sections, arrangements and scenes, the user presets and the settings. See 15.

14. MIDI and USB

  • USB is class-compliant MIDI and audio: the master output reaches the computer as a USB audio input.
  • MIDI IN over USB and the TRS jack. ROUTE decides which track each channel plays (see 13). Incoming notes on an armed track are recorded like the keys.
  • MIDI OUT (over USB; MENU > CONTROL > MIDI OUT, 0.6.72), each track on its channel (T1 = channel 1 … T6 = channel 6):
  • KEYS (the default): the keys send their notes, as before.
  • CLOCK: and the clock (24 a beat) with START / STOP, while the tempo is the FM-1's own (CLOCK INT): a drum machine or a DAW follows it. The clock runs while stopped too, so the follower knows the tempo before PLAY.
  • ALL: and every note the FM-1 plays: the sequencer, the arp, PLAYHEADS and the punch-in repeats, with the punch-ins' OCT− / OCT+ and RANDOM on them; each note gets its note-off, and STOP, a panic or a change of the setting end whatever is still on. Record the FM-1's music into a DAW as MIDI, or play other synths from it.
  • OFF: nothing.
  • PROGRAM CHANGE (0.6.72): program n on the channel a track plays loads the PRESETS list's sound number n + 1 (every engine's presets in their order, then the user presets) into that track; the selection stays where it was. (0.9.9) The engines in 0.9.8's order, ANALOG, FM6, PHASE, MATH (LOFI's number, V10.6h), VOICE, WHEEL, NOISE, DRUM, ANIMAL, whatever the PRESETS page shows (it starts with DRUM-X now): a MIDI setup's program numbers keep loading the sounds they did. Programs reach the list's first 128 sounds: since 0.8.3 that ends inside ANIMAL's presets (it comes last), so the user presets after it are picked on the device or from the editor.
  • Sections and projects (0.6.80): channel 16 is the song's: its PROGRAM CHANGE never loads a sound (whatever ROUTE). Programs 0–3 jump to section A–D of the project (playing: on the next bar, as the song layer's keys; stopped: at once; an empty one is said so); programs 16–21 load project 1–6 with its section A (stopped only, as SAVE > SECTIONS). A foot controller or a DAW can run a set.
  • Not possible: MIDI OUT on the TRS jack (the FM-1's jack is an input only: no output line goes to it). Bluetooth MIDI works since V10.12: see Bluetooth MIDI and PROX.
  • Clock: GLO > GLOBAL > CLOCK USB or TRS follows that input's clock and Start / Continue / Stop. With an external clock, TAP and GLO + PLAY restart are disabled.
  • Controllers (on the track the channel plays): CC5 glide, CC7 level, CC10 pan, CC71 resonance and CC74 cutoff (the engine's own, where it has them), CC72 release, CC73 attack, CC75 decay, CC91 reverb send, CC93 chorus send, CC94 delay send; CC16–23 the engine's own eight values (EDIT 1 / EDIT 2's knobs, E1–E8, whatever the engine: CC16 is KNOB 1 of EDIT 1), CC102 / CC103 the post filter's cutoff / resonance, CC104 drive, CC105 sustain (0.6.96). CC1 (mod wheel), CC11 (expression) and channel aftertouch are sources in the MOD matrix (MODW, EXPR, AT). Pitch bend, sustain (CC64), All Sound Off (CC120) and All Notes Off (CC123) work. A CC is recorded as automation like a knob move.
  • NRPN (V10.6z3): every parameter automation can record (the sound, the mix, the effects, E1–E8 of any engine) and the globals (tempo, swing, the buses, the SLICER, the DJ FILTER ..; not the clock, routing, loads or the engine switch) have an NRPN, with up to 14 bits of resolution. CC99 0 + CC98 the parameter's number on the channel the track plays (channel 16 also with ROUTE CH1-6: the selected track); CC99 1 + CC98 the global's number, on channel 16 (or any channel the FM-1 hears); then CC6 (and CC38 for the fine part) the value, over the parameter's whole range. The numbers and ranges: docs/NRPN.md (made from the firmware itself). The CCs above stay as they were. With MIDI OUT ALL, turning a knob on the panel sends its NRPN (a track's on its channel, a global's on channel 16): a DAW records the moves and plays them back to the FM-1.
  • For apps (V10.6z3): the editor protocol (SysEx, web/EDITOR_PROTOCOL.md) is a versioned contract (1.0) that only grows; an app can also follow the transport, the punch-ins and each MUTATE as they happen, with no MIDI OUT setting.
  • USB MIDI and audio name: "FM-1" (since V10, 2026-10-30, so the apps made for the FM-1 find it; 0.9.12–V9 "JIANT", 0.7.0–0.9.11 "JIANT FM"; "FM-1 Update" while a package installs). In a DAW the MIDI port is "FM-1": Ableton Live and other Windows programs list "FM-1" (a second one "2- FM-1"), macOS the device "FM-1" and its port "FM-1", Linux "FM-1 MIDI 1". Up to 0.9.11 Windows could keep showing "Felucca" (it remembered the first name it saw); from 0.9.12 it reads the name again. If macOS still shows an old name, open Audio MIDI Setup > MIDI Studio with the FM-1 unplugged, delete its old entry, and plug it in again. The web editor and the installer also accept a device still named "JIANT", "JIANT FM" or "Felucca".

SCL › SCENE (SCL, then SCL again until SCENE, or SELECT). Each track gets a role; with SCENE ON every key you play reaches all the tracks with a role at once, each in its own way, all from that one note and the key (SCL):

LEAD the key an octave up

  • The page shows the six tracks, their roles and the notes the last key made on each.
  • SCENE and the roles are the session's: they start OFF at power-on.

Bluetooth MIDI and PROX (V10.13–V10.20)

  • Connect: MENU › SYSTEM › BLUETOOTH ON (with the music stopped the first time). The row shows ADV (waiting) and CONN once an app is connected. Connect from a Bluetooth MIDI app on the phone or computer (it appears as JIANT FM-1); keep the app in the foreground, phones cut Bluetooth for apps in the background.
  • MIDI in: what the app sends plays the FM-1 exactly as USB-MIDI does (notes, CC, program change, bend, clock with CLOCK USB +BT, MIDI LEARN and MAP). MIDI out: what MENU › CONTROL › MIDI OUT lets out (KEYS / CLOCK / ALL) goes to the app too. No SysEx (the editor and updates stay on USB).
  • PROX: a modulation source from how close the connected phone is (its Bluetooth signal, filtered). It follows the phone, not a hand. On the BLUETOOTH row (CONN) press OCT+: the PROX page. Phone away (a metre or so): OCT− (FAR). Phone on the FM-1, top left: OCT+ (NEAR). Then on a track's MOD page pick SRC PROX (e.g. DEST CUT). It fades to 0 when the phone disconnects. The page also shows the link (L1, packets read / received) and the raw signal words.
  • AIR (V10.35, an experiment): the third value of the BLUETOOTH row. The radio stops advertising (no MIDI) and only listens to every Bluetooth device around: phones, watches, headphones, TVs. PROX then follows how strongly they are heard, which a body or a hand near the antenna (top left of the back) shadows, so no phone is needed in your hand. On the BLUETOOTH row (AIR) press OCT+, calibrate FAR (away) and NEAR (hand on it) as above. It needs some Bluetooth devices nearby; with none it stays at 0.

USB HOST: a keyboard straight in (V10.41, experimental)

MENU › SYSTEM › USB › HOST, then close the MENU: the FM-1's USB port becomes the host of a class compliant USB-MIDI keyboard or controller (no computer in between), alone or behind a hub. What it plays reaches the FM-1 as USB-MIDI does: notes, CC, program change, bend, the clock and start / stop (CLOCK USB +BT), MIDI LEARN and MAP. - Power: the FM-1 does not power the port. Use a keyboard with its own power supply, or put a powered USB hub between them. - Messages: USB HOST: PLUG A MIDI KEYBOARD, USB MIDI KEYBOARD READY, NOT A USB MIDI KEYBOARD (a device without MIDI input for the FM-1), LOW SPEED: NOT SUPPORTED, USB KEYBOARD LOST (it reconnects by itself). - While HOST is on there is no USB to a computer: no editor, no stems, no MIDI to the computer. Any other USB value turns it off. HOST is never saved: the FM-1 always powers on as a USB device, so the installer and the editor always work. - Input only: nothing is sent to the keyboard. Its code lives in the Bluetooth area of the flash (a broken install says USB HOST: INSTALL AGAIN). From Melodee (Kerem Kilic; the USB host enumeration by recursivefieldinstruments).

MIDI LEARN (0.9.12)

Any knob of a track's sound to any CC of your controller, on the device (from Felucca 1.5's MIDI LEARN):

  1. MENU › CONTROL › LEARN: SEL TRACK (the CC follows whichever track is selected when it comes) or FIXED TRK (the CC stays on the track it was learned on). Hold HOME, ALGORITHM to the CONTROL tab, SELECT to LEARN, then HOME to close the menu: learning starts.
  2. Turn a knob on any page that edits a track's parameter (HOME, EDIT, ENV, LFO, FX, the MIXER, GLO's levels): the screen says LEARN T2 CUT: SEND A CC (or its CC if it already has one).
  3. Move the controller's knob (send the CC): CC21 > T2 CUT. From then on that CC sets T2's CUT as the knob does (0–127 over its range, recorded as automation like a knob), on any channel and over USB or the TRS jack.
  4. Turn the next knob, send the next CC … LEARN OFF when you are done (with LEARN on, knobs keep picking).
  • One CC sets one parameter; a parameter has one CC: learning it again replaces the old one. Ten at most (LEARN FULL: clear one by learning its CC to another knob, or everything with LEARN CLEAR ALL).
  • Never learned (CC7 RESERVED, and they keep doing their own): CC0 / 32 (bank select), CC1 (mod wheel), CC6 / 38 / 96–101 (data entry, RPN), CC11 (expression), CC64 (sustain), CC120–127 (channel modes) and every CC of the standard map above (CC5 7 10 16–23 71–75 91 93 94 102–105). So a learned CC and the map never meet.
  • Kept with the settings (not in a project; a settings backup carries it); MENU › CONTROL › LEARN CLEAR ALL (when the MENU closes) forgets them all. ROUTE still decides which channels are heard (CH1-6 ignores channels 7–16).
  • MENU › CONTROL › MIDI MAP decides what the CCs do: FACTORY the standard map alone, CUSTOM ADD the map and your learned CCs (learning with FACTORY switches to it), CUSTOM your learned CCs alone. Learned CCs work over USB, the TRS jack and Bluetooth.

Stems (0.7.1)

Two ways to get each track on its own, to post-process them one by one in a DAW:

  • Over USB, live: MENU > SYSTEM > USB > STEMS. The device re-enumerates as an 8-channel USB audio input at 44.1 kHz, 16 bit (set the DAW's input to the device, 8 channels): channels 1–6 are the six tracks, each mono with its LEVEL, DIST, SLICER routing and mutes in and its PAN left out (what it adds to the mix, L + R halved); channels 7–8 are the FX return in stereo (the DELAY, REVERB and CHORUS buses, the SLICER's when it is on). All before the master: no leveler, limiter, CLIP or MASTER knob on them, so the sum of the eight is the mix as the buses hear it. USB LEVEL at −6 dB or lower gives headroom. The serial console is off in this mode; USB-MIDI, the editor and the installer still work. The speaker and the headphones play the master as always.

0.9.8: clean stems. Until 0.9.7 the 8-channel endpoint said 720 bytes (45 frames) while the FM-1 sends 46 frames whenever its clock gets ahead (often: it runs a little fast): the host dropped each such packet and paused, the FM-1's ring filled and whole blocks of 32 frames were lost, about a hundred times a second, in every channel at once: clicks, and a chopped, "separated by AI" sound in each stem (the stereo mix modes were not affected). Now 736 bytes; the ring also keeps a millisecond more in hand in STEMS and STEMS+IN, and a frame repeated after an underrun is made up spread over a packet instead of skipped. Update the FM-1 and the app (JIANT Stems 1.1), and record again: a take made with 0.9.7 cannot be mended afterwards. A track through the SLICER now has its own sound as the SLICER let it through in its stem (its repeats in the first such track's stem, or with the SLICER's FX ON in the FX return), so the eight still add up to the mix (until 0.9.7 that track was in its stem dry and again, sliced, in the FX return). What a stem is: exactly what the track adds to the mix in that take, so it nulls against the track rendered alone in the same take. The things that make one track depend on the others are in it, as in the mix: DUCK (the kick's pump on the other tracks), and the shared voices (8 among all tracks; LOAD ECO: 6, 4 a track, and with the load budget tight the tails already under −50 dB are trimmed): a note another track's note took the voice from ends early (a 6 ms fade). In the mix that is masked; soloed, a dense song can sound gated. For stems, record at LOAD FULL when the CPU allows it, and keep the song's polyphony within the budget. - From a backup, offline: the emulator page's "Stems". Back up the FM-1 (the editor's or the installer's "Back up"), open the emulator page, unfold "Stems", load the backup file, pick a song (its rows play once; a LOOP song once round) or the live section with a bar count, and press Render. Each track is rendered alone with its own share of the DELAY, REVERB and CHORUS (the other tracks' sends left out), plus the whole MIX (the master as the FM-1 plays it), all the same length, 16-bit 44.1 kHz stereo, in one ZIP: T1.wav .. T6.wav and MIX.wav (a silent track is left out). Faster than real time, no USB limits, nothing on the device.

USB audio in (0.7.5, test version)

MENU > SYSTEM > USB > IN+OUT makes the FM-1 a USB audio output of the computer or the phone as well as an input: the device re-enumerates (as with STEMS, the serial console is off), and the host now lists "JIANT FM" as a speaker, stereo, 16 bit, 44.1 kHz. Whatever the host plays to it comes into the last track (T6, or T4 with TRACKS 4) and is mixed like that track's sound:

  • its LEVEL sets the input's level, its PAN works as a balance, its CHORUS, DELAY and REVERB sends take the input into the effects (from the left + right sum), DUCK ducks it, its MUTE and the mute layer silence it;
  • the track's own engine still plays (an empty pattern leaves the input alone); its DIST and filter are not on the input;
  • the recording side is the stereo master as with STEREO, with the input in it.

Choose the FM-1 as the output in the host's sound settings (or in the DAW / the app), set 44.1 kHz, and play. The FM-1 follows the host's clock (it reads the audio a little faster or slower, ~3.6 ms behind); if the host stops sending, the input fades out and comes back when it resumes. Some hosts make a newly connected USB audio device the default output: that is why IN+OUT is a choice of its own, not on by default. Test version: tell us how it behaves with your computer or phone.

Stems with a voice (0.8.0, test version)

MENU > SYSTEM > USB > STEMS+IN is STEMS and IN+OUT together, for the phone app: the phone's microphone goes out to the FM-1, comes into the last track as IN+OUT's input does (its LEVEL, PAN, MUTE and its CHORUS, DELAY and REVERB sends: your voice through that track's effects), and 10 channels come back: T1–T6, the FX return L R, then the voice as the track mixed it and the voice as it came. The voice has a channel of its own (not in the track's stem), so the app can move it earlier by the round trip's latency and the voice lands in time with what you heard; the second voice channel is the reference the app measures that latency against. The voice's share of the FX return cannot be moved (it is mixed with every track's): its reverb and delays stay the latency late (~20–30 ms: in a reverb it sounds like pre-delay). The phone app reads and writes the FM-1 with its own USB driver (Android passes only some of a USB device's channels to apps). Test version: the app is JIANT Probe for now (its test 7).

16. Troubleshooting and FAQ

The installer does not install: what to do

Installer: https://juanjiant-bit.github.io/JIANT/webapp/installer/. When something fails it shows a What to do box with the steps for that error; if that is not enough, press COPY REPORT and paste it into your message (the browser, the MIDI ports it saw, the FM-1's firmware, the step it stopped at).

  • "This browser has no WebMIDI": Chrome or Edge on a computer. iPhone and iPad cannot (no browser there has WebMIDI); Safari has none; Android Chrome with an OTG cable may work, a computer is safer.
  • MIDI not allowed / blocked: the icon left of the address → Site settings → MIDI devices → Allow; reload. Brave: Shields off for the site.
  • FM-1 not found: switch it on; a USB data cable (many only charge); straight into the computer, no hub. Then try Is it in boot mode (WL80UBOOT)?.
  • Another program is using its MIDI port (Windows: one program per port): close the M-VAVE app, the DAW, MIDI-OX and other tabs with the JIANT editor; replug, reload, press Install.
  • An older official firmware (V14, V13…): Return to official (V16 or V15, no backup needed), then Install.
  • Felucca, Skiff or another custom firmware: Return to official first. It saves that firmware's backup first (Felucca's with its samples; Skiff's in its own format, for Skiff's editor) or, when it cannot make one, asks before going on without; then Install.
  • Where is FM-1.fwsc? M-VAVE's download page (https://www.m-vave.com/download): FM-1, firmware V16 (what it offers now), unzip it, the file FM-1.fwsc (718,196 bytes; V15's, if you kept it, is 699,956 bytes). We cannot host it: it is M-VAVE's. Picking the ZIP, another version or a JIANT package is told apart.
  • Stopped half way (update mode not found, stopped, connection lost): do not switch it off; replug and press the same button: it resumes. Windows may take a minute to install the update mode's driver the first time.
  • Black screen: connect it and press Install again; WL80UBOOT is the chip's boot mode (another cable, straight in; else FM-1 Transporter).
  • Page not found: use the address above exactly; old links (/JIANT/installer/, /JIANT/webapp/, a link with :1:1 stuck to it) now redirect by themselves.

The firmware

MENU › SYSTEM › BLUETOOTH says MISSING (was NO SEG), or "BLUETOOTH PART MISSING: REINSTALL: JIANT INSTALLER". The Bluetooth part of JIANT (it also holds GEN, the songs' saving, USB HOST and more) was not installed: reinstall with the web installer (above). Other flashing tools write only the main firmware.

My songs are not kept after power-off (a newer FM-1). FM-1s sold from late 2026 (the logo on the front) have a different flash chip (JEDEC 0xB36014). JIANT before V10.6z did not know it and ran from RAM only: nothing was saved, GENE was missing and the device could not update itself. Install the current version (the installer writes from the computer, so it works on those units): from then on it saves normally.

Songs 7 and 8 are gone. Since V10.6p JIANT keeps 6 songs (projects; the flash of 7 and 8 is kept for a drum sample bank). An FM-1 left on song 7 or 8 opens on song 6, with a one-time notice if they held music. A backup made before updating still holds them: move what you need into 1–6 with an earlier version, or keep the file.

It says SAFE START. A start that hung was reset, and this start skipped the power setup (V10.46). It plays on one core then (V10.6z2: the second core starts only on the stock power settings); the next normal power-on is back to two.

T5 or T6 is silent. TRACKS is set to 4 (the default): T5 and T6 are asleep. GLO > SYSTEM > TRACKS 6 (or MENU > AUDIO > TRACKS). The screen tells you when you play into an asleep track.

After clearing a sequence, notes still played on all tracks. This was fixed in v0.6.28: a clear now ends the punch-in lane's repeats (STUTTER, ARP), a DRUM track with no steps gets no ARP fill, and a section jump or quick chain waiting for the bar is called off. If something still plays after a clear, it may be the punch-in lane: hold REC and press FX to erase it (or SEQ + REC to clear everything).

Two cores (0.6.71) and MENU › SYSTEM › CPU CORES (0.9.12). The second core renders some synth tracks; MENU › SYSTEM › ABOUT says CORES 2, or CORES 1 and why. Since 0.9.12 MENU › SYSTEM › CPU CORES chooses 2 CORES (the default) or 1 CORE, kept with the settings, from the next start. If the FM-1 crashes or hangs while both cores work, or the second core misreads, stops answering or faults, it turns 2 CORES off for good and says so (2 CORES OFF AFTER A CRASH / CORE 2 FAILED: 1 CORE, then MENU › SYSTEM › CPU CORES); ABOUT then reads CORES 1 (AFTER A CRASH). Set 2 CORES again only if you want to test it. Hold REC while powering on for one core that session only. With both cores running the tracks share 12 voices instead of 8 (each track still at most 8); on one core (1 CORE set, after a crash, REC held) it is 8, and a second core that stops brings the budget back to 8 from the next note.

It crashes: a red screen "JIANT CRASH" (or "FELUCCA CRASH" before 0.7.0) with numbers. Take a photo of it and tell us the version (MENU › SYSTEM › ABOUT). How to read it:

  • 0.9.12 on: the version, CORE 0 OF 2 (the core that faulted, of how many were running), CODE xx-nn (the cause and the vector, below), then VEC, PC, EMU, DBG, RETS.
  • Before 0.9.12 the same five numbers came unlabelled, in that order, top to bottom: VEC (1 = the CPU exception), PC (where it failed), EMU and DBG (the cause bits), RETS (who called the function that failed).
  • The code: IL illegal instruction, MA misaligned access, DZ divide by zero, ST stack overflow, PC a jump outside the code, WR a write into a protected area, MM the memory unit, BU an access to no memory (a bad pointer), WD a hang (the watchdog reset it: no red screen), ?? none of those.
  • The FM-1 restarts by itself after 4 s. The last crash stays in the settings, also after switching off: MENU > ABOUT > LAST CRASH shows it as one line (IL01 2C 0.9.12 0201ABCD 0201ABCD: code and vector, cores, version, PC, RETS). The editor's settings backup carries it too.
  • For developers: tools/crash_decode.py VEC PC EMU DBG RETS names the function, against the ELF of the version that crashed.

What to try when it crashes now and then (even idle). In this order, a few hours each: 1. MENU › SYSTEM › CPU CORES 1 CORE (0.9.12 does it by itself after the first crash with both cores; before 0.9.12, hold REC at every power-on). 2. MENU › SYSTEM › CPU CLOCK 320 MHZ: the chip at JieLi's rated clock instead of the 360 MHz the FM-1's boot sets (switched at the next power-on; ABOUT shows CPU 320 MHZ). About 11 % less CPU: a heavy song may shed voices sooner (LOAD LITE helps). If the FM-1 refuses (its clock is set up differently), ABOUT keeps CPU 360 MHZ. 3. If it still crashes: the photo, the version and LAST CRASH, please.

Clicks, stutters, the load meter full. Keep TRACKS on 4, try LOAD LITE or ECO, use fewer voices (MONO, fewer FM6 tracks), lighter reverbs (CLOUD and SHIMMER are the heaviest). Check MENU > SYSTEM > ABOUT > AUDIO LOAD.

SELECT doesn't change the tempo. Since 0.6.67 SELECT alone scrolls the pages; the tempo is GLO held + SELECT (or GLO's F4 TAP, or GLO > GLOBAL's BPM knob).

The knobs control M1–M4 instead of the page. The macros are latched (header [LFO] LATCH). Press LFO.

A song won't start: "PATTERN A EMPTY" / "ADD A SONG ROW". A row points to an empty section, or there are no rows. Save music into that section, or change the row.

It says SONG PLAYS: STOP IT TO EDIT. A song (SONG mode, SAVE + D5, or ARRANGE / TIMELINE PLAY) or a quick chain is playing; stop it to edit steps, use GEN, EUCLID, tools or UNDO (or turn SONG mode off with SAVE + D5 so PLAY plays the loop).

The keys feel inverted / a button does the wrong thing. Run HARDWARE CALIBRATION: hold OCT− and OCT+ while powering on (or MENU > SYSTEM > CALIBRATION) and teach each button and knob. A piano key cancels.

A new USB mode doesn't show up on the phone or computer. Since 0.9.10 the FM-1 leaves the bus for half a second when MENU › SYSTEM › USB changes (it came back after ~25 ms, and some phones and hubs did not notice it had gone, so they kept the old mode); it appears about a second after the menu closes. Older firmware: switch again, or unplug.

SAVED (NO FLASH). The flash chip wasn't detected; saves stay in RAM only. Power-cycle the device. If it persists, back up what you can with the editor and reinstall.

The FM-1 stays black after an interrupted update. Check whether the computer sees it as WL80UBOOT (a USB device 4C4A:8057): that's the chip's boot mode and the installer can write it again (try another cable). To enter update mode deliberately from a running device, hold OCT− + OCT+ for 5 seconds. If that isn't enough, FM-1 Transporter reads and writes the flash with a Seeed XIAO RP2040.

I want the factory firmware back. Use the installer's Return to official section with M-VAVE's V16 or V15 (it backs up first).
