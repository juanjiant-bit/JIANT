JIANT beyond the FM-1

JIANT FM is meant to run whole, on its own, on more than one machine: the M-VAVE FM-1, the M-VAVE SMK-37 Pro / Elite and the Akai MPC Sample, plus a plugin for the MPC standalones and desktop hosts. Every one of them plays the same firmware sources (firmware/src), so a song sounds the same everywhere.

Full power on a bigger CPU

The FM-1's chip is small, and JIANT lives within it: when the load runs high it gives way (STRING, WEST and VOICE at half the rate, FM6 at a quarter, the heavy reverbs as lighter ones, voices faded), all parts share 8 voices, and EXPLORE keeps its pieces light. The ports run on far bigger CPUs, so the core builds with FELUCCA_HOST_POWER (firmware/src/core.h; the FM-1, the browser emulator and the host tests are untouched by it):

  • Every track its own 8 voices (48 in all), not 8 between all of them.
  • EXPLORE at full size: no lighter pieces for the FM-1's CPU.

  • The load measured in real time on that CPU (web/emu/felucca_web.c JIANT_REAL_LOAD): the firmware's own governor and overload guard stay, so the sound gives way only if that CPU itself runs short; on one with room it never does. jiant_cpu_percent() and friends report it; the MPC Sample app shows it on the screen (CPU, red when anything gave way), in status, and jiant --bench measures the bench song on the MPC's own CPU.

  • Samples (firmware/src/smp_slots.c, the API in jiant_core.h): 16 slots of up to 60 s, mono or stereo, loaded from the host's PCM at any rate (made and resampled on the host's own thread, handed to the audio thread by pointer, freed by jiant_service on the host's service thread), recorded from the device's input (jiant_input), JIANT's master or a track, and played by two engines in the FM-1's retired slots, SAMPLE (4: one shot with experimental modes and triggers, a feedback comb, DESTROY, REC) and GRAIN (8: granular, on a slot or LIVE on the input or the master, time-stretch, feedback and shimmer), and by a sample layer on every DRUM-X sound (SMORPH and its blends). The controls: mpc-sample/README.md. On the FM-1 none of it exists: a project made with them plays SAMPLE and GRAIN as ANALOG there (the retired engines' way) and DRUM-X as its synthesized kit (the layer's values sit in slots a DRUM track there never reads).

On this computer (x86) the bench song renders at ~37x real time; on the MPC Sample's Cortex-A55 a share of one of its four cores, to be measured with --bench on the hardware.

The core

ports/core/build.sh test      # build/core/libjiant_core.a, then its test

One device per library (the firmware keeps its state in globals), not thread safe; see the header. For several in one process (JIANT FM on several tracks of a DAW), jiant_copies.h: the core compiled N times (jiant_copy.c, 8 in the JUCE plugin), each copy one translation unit with every external symbol renamed (the list, copy_symbols.h, is made from the core's object by ports/core/build.sh symbols and checked by the test), its API a table of functions; an instance claims a free copy and gives it back. The copies share nothing, so each may run on its own thread. Plain C and the preprocessor: no object tools, the same on Linux, Windows (clang-cl) and macOS. The test boots it, plays a MIDI note, draws the screen, plays a generated song, saves and restores the music, follows an external MIDI clock, loads and records sample slots. sample_test drives the sample engines with the firmware's internals at hand: every SAMPLE mode and trigger, GRAIN's travel modes, time-stretch, feedback and LIVE input, DRUM-X's blends (and with SMORPH 0 bit for bit the FM-1's DRUM-X), REC from the master, the bounds at the engines' tops and their cost; then fm1_load_test, the core built as the FM-1 is, loads the project it saved; copies_test links 8 copies beside the single library: copy 0 plays what the single core plays, sample for sample; three copies on three threads at once play what three others play one after another, bit for bit; one copy's music and samples touch no other's.

The MPC plugin

pip install ziglang           # the cross compiler, as inMusic's SDK uses
python3 ports/mpc-plugin/build.py

It fetches inMusic's MPC Plugin SDK (MIT) at a pinned commit into build/mpc-plugin-sdk, builds the core and the plugin for the desktop, ARM32 and ARM64, runs the SDK's host simulator (the MPC's load sequence and its quirks, at 44.1 kHz and at 48 kHz), and assembles the folder the MPC installs, checked with the SDK's validate.py:

build/mpc-plugin/Juan Jiant - VST - JIANT FM/      (and a zip of it)

On an MPC: Preferences > Enhancements (Beta) > Enable Enhancements and External Plugin Support; copy the folder to VST2 Plugins on a USB drive or SD card, restart, and load JIANT FM on a plugin track.

What the plugin does:

  • MIDI: channels 1 to 4 play tracks 1 to 4 (GLO > MIDI IN ROUTE CH1-4).
  • Sync Host (default): the sequencer follows the MPC's transport and tempo (a 24 PPQN clock made from the host's beats). Internal: its own tempo; Play starts and stops it.
  • GENE: genre, seed, energy and form; Generate makes a song.
  • The music is saved in the MPC project: the live section, the songs and the user presets.
  • Any sample rate: 44.1 kHz as is (the MPC's), other rates resampled.

Limits, today:

  • No JIANT screen inside an MPC: MPC plugins show only the MPC's parameter page. The full JIANT, its screen and its UI, is the MPC Sample app's job (mpc-sample/), not the plugin's.
  • One JIANT per project: a second instance stays silent (the firmware is one device).
  • Tried in the SDK's host simulator, not yet on MPC hardware.
