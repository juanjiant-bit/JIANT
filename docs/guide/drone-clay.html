DRONE CLAY

A sequencer that does not line up notes: it kneads sound. A drone with plasticity, a malleable mass of frequency that stretches, gets struck and transforms without ever stopping.

The idea

The usual sequencer thinks of time as a row of boxes: each holds a note or nothing. DRONE CLAY starts from the opposite. Sound comes first, continuous, deep, already there; time does not create it, it shapes it.

The drone is the clay: a single very low note, played with the track's own voice, that can be held forever. Four hands work that mass, the modulators. Each presses a different place (the filter, the pitch, the waveform, the pan, an effect send) with its own gesture: a dry hit, a slow drift, a staircase, a bounce, an arpeggio on the scale. What you hear is the imprint of those hands on the material.

You do not write a melody: you give form to a mass of frequency. The pattern is not a list of events, it is a pressure that returns.

That is why the mass is never the same twice. The modulators have their own rhythms (shapes, euclideans, amounts, decays) and cross at different phases: the clay settles into a state and the next hit pulls it out. Bio-generative in JIANT's sense: simple rules, living material, a result that evolves.

The parts

The drone

A low note in the track's voice. Pick note and octave. RATE at HOLD holds it forever; any other value (1/64 to 4BAR, dotted and triplets too) strikes it again with the tempo. PITCH moves it −24 to +36 semitones.

Four modulators

On CLAY MOD, MOD picks which of the four you edit: DEST (which track parameter it kneads), SHAPE (the gesture) and AMOUNT (how much; at 0 it strikes nothing). On CLAY RHYTHM, its own RATE (its grid), EUCLID (when it presses) and DECAY.

Hits

The first shapes are drum hits made from the mass itself: KICK, SNARE, HAT, CLAP, TOM, ZAP… The drone turns percussive without ceasing to be a drone.

Slow shapes

SINE, DRIFT, SPRING, STAIRS, BOUNCE, CHAOS, S&H, ramps and envelopes: stretch, loosen, let fall.

Note gestures

ARP, CHORD, +OCT, +5TH, GLIDE DN play on the project's scale; the FM x½ … x7 shapes change the harmonic colour.

Memory of the imprint

DECAY (on CLAY RHYTHM) sets how long each hit takes to let go: short is a pinch, long is a mark that stays. On CLAY TIME, SEED changes the variation and RUN chooses whether it follows the song (SONG) or runs free (FREE); with NOKEY the keys play notes as usual.

Quick start

  1. Pick a track and go to SEQ › tab CLAY.
  2. On DRONE CLAY set ON, pick note and octave, and RATE at HOLD to start with the mass still.
  3. On CLAY MOD, with MOD at 1: DEST to CUTOFF, SHAPE to KICK and raise AMOUNT; on CLAY RHYTHM give it a EUCLID and a RATE until the hit is felt.
  4. Add MOD 2 with a slow shape (DRIFT or SINE) on SHAPE or PAN: the mass starts to breathe.
  5. With MOD 3 on a note gesture (ARP UP, CHORD m) the clay sings on the scale.
  6. Set DECAY (CLAY RHYTHM) so the imprints fade fast or stay.
  7. Play the white keys: each strikes a gesture on the MOD picked (OCT− / OCT+ change the group of gestures); the last seven keys pick the drone's degree on the scale.

Good to know

  • With MONO or LEGATO voicing, when a sequence step plays the drone yields the voice and comes back after. In POLY both sound.
  • A time or pitch punch-in restarts the mass from its first hit; on release it returns to the grid.
  • It works per track: six different masses, each with its own engine, can be kneaded at once.
  • It is saved with the project. Old projects open with DRONE CLAY off.
